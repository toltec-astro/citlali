Compare numerical map values, support, relevant coefficients/normalization, and source measurements—not merely the peak. Explain discrepancies against justified tolerances. A material unexplained mismatch blocks interpretation of the nonzero-feedback result.
This is an integration control, not a no-source observation or a false-positive test.
Subtraction/rejoin accounting. With residual processing bypassed in a suitable fixture, removal and rejoin must recover the original input on the declared support. Test signs, units, sample alignment, and missing-support behavior.
Also characterize the actual projected-and-remapped model contribution. Do not assume that projection followed by mapping leaves the input candidate image unchanged.

Compare the relevant cleaning action or subspace, not raw eigenvector signs or ordering. The real observation need not show a large subspace change for the implementation to be correct.
Replacement semantics. A small fixture must demonstrate that previously admitted content can be removed when it is absent from the new reconstructed total. The implementation must not union old and new masks or retain rejected pixels through an implicit carry-forward operation.
Include a small known-source synthetic case exercising the numerical projection/PTC/rejoin/MAP route, rather than relying only on bookkeeping mocks. Keep it small and interpretable; this is not a broad injection campaign or a requirement to establish final calibration accuracy.
Follow the existing test and independent-review requirements for the actual changed scope.
