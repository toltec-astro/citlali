**I still recommend addressing the common source fitter next, but my previous explanation overstated what the fixed-template result proves.** The next experiment should repair and test the measurement—not try to reproduce the favorable 11/12 result or rescue starlet.

## Reassessment

**The two readouts measure different quantities.** The free Gaussian estimates the peak of its best-fitting, freely shaped profile. The fixed-template fit estimates the coefficient multiplying the known injected shape, while also fitting a background. Those quantities agree for an exactly recovered Gaussian, but need not agree for a distorted source.

For example, a narrower recovered source could have the correct central peak but a deficient coefficient against the original, broader template. That would be a real profile discrepancy, but not necessarily an erroneous free-Gaussian peak. Conversely, a nearly correct template coefficient does not establish that the rest of the reconstructed morphology is correct.

Thus, the reported **0/12 versus 11/12** comparison is strong evidence of a difference in *amplitude measured against the injected profile*. It is not, by itself, proof that starlet preserves the entire source better, or that repairing the Gaussian fitter should reproduce 11/12.

**The six lower-cost alternatives provide the decisive numerical evidence.** According to the supplied report, these are feasible members of the same Gaussian-plus-plane family, evaluated on identical pixels. They demonstrate that the saved fitter returned solutions inferior to known alternatives. They do not yet identify whether the cause is initialization, parameter scaling, derivatives, premature termination, or selection among local minima. A nonlinear least-squares solver’s success flag does not certify a global optimum; its termination can reflect small steps or small objective changes rather than the requested gradient condition. 

**The remaining crossed-noise errors are not automatically a FRUIT defect.** They survive removal of the free-shape fitting ambiguity, but could reflect residual map noise, background coupling, noise-dependent cleaning response, or a mixture. Two reused noise realizations cannot distinguish those explanations. My earlier suggestion to treat that remainder as belonging straightforwardly to FRUIT/PTC was too strong.

The most targeted repair is also more specific than “try more starts.” **For any fixed Gaussian geometry, amplitude and the three background-plane coefficients are linear parameters.** Solve those four coefficients directly, and reserve nonlinear optimization for centroid, widths, and orientation. This is the standard *variable-projection* approach to separable nonlinear least squares. It reduces the nonlinear search without fixing the source shape or changing the objective, provided the original constraints are preserved. 

That gives us the following directive.
