## Evidence and scope

The 152389 repeat spent 167.52 s in saved-input loading across health and six
weighted map passes. An earlier native M0–M5 profile attributed 3,988 of
18,873 main-thread MAP samples to nested canonical APT verification. Those
samples overlap loader samples and come from an earlier application. The
current source fully interpreted the observation's common bundle and admitted
its detector relation once per network on each pass.

This Tier 2 increment changes that repeated interpretation within a single
weighted native invocation. The authoritative APT reader admits the complete
fresh v2 bundle, including its embedded baseline. The native stage holds one
immutable selection and relation. On each reuse the reader compares every
member's actual bytes, including root receipt and manifest, and checks for
missing, nonregular, symlinked, aliased or unexpected entries. Network-specific
manifest digest, observation, channel, array, row, units and CAL bindings still
apply. A change of locator triggers a new full admission. Before replacing the
current entry, the owner rechecks it. At stage publication the owner verifies
every locator used. Evicted locators retain only their original root manifest
digest and receipt bytes. Fresh canonical readmission at publication enforces
those bindings and the complete declared member inventory, one bundle at a time.
Final source-ledger verification also runs. Other saved MAP users keep one
full admission per network.

The stage receipt records full admissions, exact-byte network reuses, selection
wall time and final recheck. The 152389 expectation is one full admission and
ten reuses on each of seven passes. CAL values, slots, masks, coordinates,
numerical order, seeds, feedback and products retain their existing owners.
Cross-pass CAL reuse is a later decision, guided by the new timing.
