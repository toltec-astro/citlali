\begin{abstract}
This document derives the statistical contract for
\code{post-processing.map-filter.type: convolve} independently of the
candidate implementation.  Its starting point is an affine estimator, not a
particular C++ formula.  The derivation separates stored filtered amplitude,
response-corrected sky or template amplitude, statistical inverse variance,
and dimensionless support or confidence.  It then audits the baseline
application and the two-commit correction candidate, identifies the remaining
scientific choices, recommends a product contract, and specifies the evidence
required before integration or fruit-loop use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Executive summary}
\label{sec:summary}

For a deterministic affine filter
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol y = A\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol c,
  \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
the stored filtered amplitude has covariance
\begin{equation}
  C_y=A C_x A^{\mathsf T}.
  \label{eq:affine-cov}
\end{equation}
For independent input pixels with formal variances $V_j$, its diagonal is
\begin{equation}
  V_{y,i}=\sum_j A_{ij}^2V_j.
  \label{eq:diagonal-propagation}
\end{equation}
Every input that enters the signal with a nonzero stochastic coefficient must
therefore enter formal variance with the square of that same coefficient.
Low positive input weight means high variance; it does not mean invalid
support.

For a fixed, globally unit-sum convolution with a binary input edge window
$T_j$, a binary output window $O_i$, and deterministic fill $b_j$, the
independently derived stored product is
\begin{align}
 y_i &= O_i\sum_j k_{ij}\{T_jx_j+(1-T_j)b_j\},
 \label{eq:fixed-edge-signal}\\
 V_{y,i} &= O_i^2\sum_j k_{ij}^2T_j^2V_j,
 \label{eq:fixed-edge-var}\\
 W_{y,i} &= V_{y,i}^{-1}\quad(V_{y,i}>0\hbox{ and valid}).
 \label{eq:fixed-edge-weight}
\end{align}
This is a fixed filtered amplitude, not a locally normalized sky estimator.
The variance sum applies only to inputs with a defined stochastic variance.
In the current candidate those are the finite positive-weight inputs; a zero
or non-finite-weight input must carry no stochastic signal or noise
contribution.
Near an edge, its response and fill contribution must be exposed rather than
hidden in a quantity called weight.  A response-corrected estimator for a
specified template $p$ is a different product,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\alpha_i={y_i-c_i\over R_i(p)},\qquad
 \Var(\widehat\alpha_i)={V_{y,i}\over R_i(p)^2},
 \label{eq:response-corrected-summary}
\end{equation}
where $R_i(p)=\sum_jA_{ij}p_j$ and $c_i$ is the deterministic affine fill.

The recommended Citlali contract is therefore the combination of options A,
D, and E in the audit request: retain the fixed, globally normalized filtered
amplitude and keep formal variance, empirical variance, response, support, and
any confidence or feedback gain as distinct products.  Until an explicit
support equation and scientific threshold are chosen and validated, filtered
products are withheld from production fruit-loop selection rather than being
masked by an invented threshold.  If photometric correction is required, add
a separately named template-specific estimator rather than relabeling the
fixed smoothing output as point-source flux.

Four terms are deliberately not interchangeable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{stored filtered amplitude}: the numerical output of the filtering
operator;
\item \emph{response-corrected estimator}: an estimate of a named sky or
template amplitude after correcting the realized filter response;
\item \emph{statistical inverse variance}: the reciprocal variance of one
explicitly named estimator; and
\item \emph{support, validity, or confidence}: a mask, fractional geometric
support, response guard, heuristic confidence, or feedback gain.
\end{enumerate}
The unqualified word ``weight'' is avoided whenever one of these meanings can
be stated instead.

\section{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}

Input map pixels are $x_j$; output pixels are $y_i$.  Their expectations are
$\mu_j$ and $\mu_{y,i}$.  $C_x$ and $C_y$ are full covariance matrices.
When the diagonal approximation is invoked, $V_j=(C_x)_{jj}$ and
$V_{y,i}=(C_y)_{ii}$.  A finite positive formal input inverse variance is
$w_j>0$ and
\begin{equation}
 V_j={1\over w_j}.
 \label{eq:input-weight-to-var}
\end{equation}
$k_{ij}$ is the fixed convolution coefficient from input $j$ to output $i$.
$T_j\in\{0,1\}$ is a binary input edge/operator window and
$O_i\in\{0,1\}$ is a binary output window.  Citlali currently realizes
$O=T$.  The corresponding diagonal matrices are also denoted $T$ and $O$.
$T$ is not, by itself, the finite-positive-weight eligibility mask: the
realized guardband can contain zero-weight pixels.
$t_j$ and $a_i$ are fractional input and output tapers.  $b_j$ is an exterior
fill.  $p_j$ is a specified sky template of unit amplitude and $\alpha$ its
amplitude.

\section{General affine estimator and covariance propagation}
\label{sec:general}

From Equation~\eqref{eq:affine}, for deterministic $A$ and $\boldsymbol c$,
\begin{align}
 \E[\boldsymbol y] &= A\E[\boldsymbol x]+\boldsymbol c,
 \label{eq:affine-mean}\\
 C_y &= \E[(\boldsymbol y-\E\boldsymbol y)
                 (\boldsymbol y-\E\boldsymbol y)^{\mathsf T}]
      =A C_x A^{\mathsf T}.
 \label{eq:affine-cov-expanded}
\end{align}
Under $C_x=\diag(V_j)$,
\begin{align}
 \Var(y_i)&=\sum_j A_{ij}^2V_j,
 \label{eq:affine-diag-var}\\
 \Cov(y_i,y_\ell)&=\sum_j A_{ij}A_{\ell j}V_j.
 \label{eq:affine-output-cov}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:affine-output-cov} is essential: convolution correlates
output pixels even when the input covariance is diagonal.  A persisted
variance plane is only $\diag(C_y)$ and cannot encode the covariance needed
for exact aperture, connected-region, or multi-pixel template significance.

If an exterior fill $\boldsymbol b$ is random rather than deterministic, write
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol y=A\boldsymbol x+D\boldsymbol b,
 \label{eq:random-fill-model}
\end{equation}
where $A=OKT$ and $D=OK(I-T)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 C_y=A C_xA^{\mathsf T}+D C_bD^{\mathsf T}
     +A C_{xb}D^{\mathsf T}+D C_{bx}A^{\mathsf T}.
 \label{eq:random-fill-cov}
\end{equation}
Omitting the last three terms is justified only when the fill is fixed and
known, or when its uncertainty and cross-covariance are demonstrably
negligible.  If $\boldsymbol b=H\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol d$ is estimated
linearly from the same map, the effective stochastic operator is
$A+DH$ and the exact covariance is
\begin{equation}
 C_y=(A+DH)C_x(A+DH)^{\mathsf T}.
 \label{eq:data-derived-fill-cov}
\end{equation}

\section{Interior fixed convolution}
\label{sec:interior}

For a fully supported fixed kernel,
\begin{equation}
 y_i=\sum_jk_{ij}x_j,
 \label{eq:interior-signal}
\end{equation}
so under diagonal input covariance
\begin{align}
 V_{y,i}&=\sum_jk_{ij}^2V_j,
 \label{eq:interior-var}\\
 \Cov(y_i,y_\ell)&=\sum_jk_{ij}k_{\ell j}V_j.
 \label{eq:interior-cov}
\end{align}
For translation-invariant $k$ and uniform white input variance $\sigma^2$,
\begin{equation}
 V_y=\sigma^2\sum_jk_j^2.
 \label{eq:white-var}
\end{equation}
If $\sum_jk_j=1$, constant fields are preserved, while
\begin{align}
 {\sigma_{\rm out}\over\sigma_{\rm in}}&=
      \sqrt{\sum_jk_j^2},
 \label{eq:white-rms-ratio}\\
 {W_{\rm out}\over W_{\rm in}}&={1\over\sum_jk_j^2}.
 \label{eq:white-weight-ratio}
\end{align}
These statements concern the stored filtered amplitude.  Unit DC gain does
not imply unit response to a delta function, a beam template, or integrated
point-source flux.

\section{Validity, low formal weight, and variable coverage}
\label{sec:validity}

Equation~\eqref{eq:input-weight-to-var} gives the decisive distinction:
$0<w_j\ll1$ is a valid, high-variance input.  If the signal coefficient
$A_{ij}$ is nonzero, its variance contribution is $A_{ij}^2/w_j$.  Retaining
the sample in signal while omitting that term is a signal/variance operator
mismatch and overstates significance.

If a policy threshold deliberately rejects a low-weight pixel, define
$M_j=\mathbf1(w_j\geq w_{\min})$ and change the complete stochastic operator:
\begin{align}
 y_i&=\sum_jA_{ij}M_jx_j+c_i,
 \label{eq:thresholded-signal}\\
 V_{y,i}&=\sum_jA_{ij}^2M_jV_j,
 \label{eq:thresholded-var}\\
 R_i(p)&=\sum_jA_{ij}M_jp_j.
 \label{eq:thresholded-response}
\end{align}
Support must be computed from the same $M_j$.  A threshold may instead define
a displayed coverage diagnostic, but then it must not silently change only
formal variance or only output validity.

\section{Binary edge support and deterministic background fill}
\label{sec:edges}

Define
\begin{equation}
 x_j^*=T_jx_j+(1-T_j)b_j
 \label{eq:edge-input}
\end{equation}
and apply the globally fixed kernel followed by the output window:
\begin{align}
 y_i&=O_i\sum_jk_{ij}x_j^*
     =O_i\sum_j k_{ij}T_jx_j+c_i,
 \label{eq:edge-stored}\\
 A_{ij}&=O_i k_{ij}T_j,
 \label{eq:edge-A}\\
 c_i&=O_i\sum_jk_{ij}(1-T_j)b_j.
 \label{eq:edge-c}
\end{align}
For deterministic fill and diagonal input covariance,
\begin{align}
 V_{y,i}&=O_i^2\sum_jk_{ij}^2T_j^2V_j,
 \label{eq:edge-var}\\
 \Cov(y_i,y_\ell)&=O_iO_\ell
     \sum_jk_{ij}k_{\ell j}T_j^2V_j.
 \label{eq:edge-cov}
\end{align}

For a globally unit-sum kernel define the realized constant-sky response
\begin{equation}
 r_i=O_i\sum_jk_{ij}T_j.
 \label{eq:edge-response}
\end{equation}
If the true sky and fill are constants $s$ and $b$,
\begin{equation}
 \E[y_i]=r_i s+(O_i-r_i)b
         =O_i b+(s-b)r_i.
 \label{eq:constant-edge-mean}
\end{equation}
Thus zero fill attenuates a constant sky by $r_i$; correct fill ($b=s$)
preserves the output-windowed sky; and the edge bias relative to $O_i s$ is
\begin{equation}
 \E[y_i]-O_i s=(O_i-r_i)(b-s).
 \label{eq:edge-bias}
\end{equation}
For a compact template $p$, its stochastic response is
\begin{equation}
 R_i(p)=O_i\sum_jk_{ij}T_jp_j.
 \label{eq:edge-template-response}
\end{equation}
Fill is an additive offset, not template response, and must be subtracted if a
response-corrected template amplitude is formed.

Full support with $T_j=1$ and $O_i=1$ gives $c_i=0$ and the interior
equations.  Partial support gives spatially varying response, variance, and
covariance.  One valid input $q$ gives $y_i=O_i k_{iq}x_q+c_i$ and
$V_{y,i}=O_i^2k_{iq}^2V_q$.  Zero valid support gives deterministic
$y_i=c_i$ and zero stochastic variance; an output with $O_i=0$ is likewise
masked.  Either case is scientifically invalid, not an infinitely precise
measurement.  This proves that validity cannot be inferred from inverse
variance alone.

\subsection{Support metrics}

For a nonnegative unit-sum kernel, $r_i$ in
Equation~\eqref{eq:edge-response} is also a useful fractional kernel support.
For a signed kernel, cancellation makes $\sum kT$ unsuitable as a geometric
support metric.  Two explicit alternatives are
\begin{align}
 q_i^{(|k|)}&={O_i\sum_j|k_{ij}|T_j\over\sum_j|k_{ij}|},
 \label{eq:l1-support}\\
 q_i^{(k^2)}&={O_i^2\sum_jk_{ij}^2T_j^2\over\sum_jk_{ij}^2}.
 \label{eq:l2-support}
\end{align}
$q^{(|k|)}$ measures retained absolute operator mass; $q^{(k^2)}$ measures
retained variance leverage under uniform input variance.  A binary-footprint
fraction measures geometry but ignores coefficient importance.  None is a
statistical inverse variance.  A future support plane must name its norm,
input-eligibility mask, output mask, and validity floor and must keep signed
template/DC response separate.  The second audit found that the current edge
window may include zero-weight guardband pixels, so neither expression is
adopted as a production-selection product in the bounded repair.  Filtered
selection that requires such a product remains withheld pending the choice
and validation of those semantics.

\section{Locally normalized alternative}
\label{sec:local-normalization}

For $r_i=O_i\sum_jk_{ij}T_j\ne0$, a locally normalized estimator is
\begin{equation}
 y_i^{\rm LN}={O_i\sum_jk_{ij}T_jx_j\over r_i}.
 \label{eq:local-signal}
\end{equation}
Its diagonal variance and covariance are
\begin{align}
 V_{i}^{\rm LN}&={O_i^2\sum_jk_{ij}^2T_j^2V_j\over r_i^2},
 \label{eq:local-var}\\
 \Cov(y_i^{\rm LN},y_\ell^{\rm LN})
 &= {O_iO_\ell\sum_jk_{ij}k_{\ell j}T_j^2V_j\over r_ir_\ell}.
 \label{eq:local-cov}
\end{align}
It preserves a constant sky where $r_i$ is well conditioned, but changes the
operator, noise, compact-source response, and covariance with position.  Near
small or cancellation-dominated $r_i$, its variance can diverge.

On an accepted output with $O_i=1$ and binary $T$, the expression
\begin{equation}
 {\sum_jk_{ij}^2T_jV_j\over\sum_jk_{ij}^2T_j}
 \label{eq:k2-mask-ratio}
\end{equation}
is not the variance of Equation~\eqref{eq:local-signal}: its denominator lacks
the square of $\sum kT$.  It would equal the variance of an estimator with
coefficients $kT/\sqrt{\sum k^2T}$, but only if the signal were normalized by
that same square-root factor.  Dividing only variance by $k^2$ support therefore
has no matching signal estimator and is a definite operator mismatch.

\section{Fractional taper or apodization}
\label{sec:taper}

For deterministic input taper $t_j$ and output taper $a_i$,
\begin{align}
 y_i&=a_i\sum_jk_{ij}t_jx_j,
 \label{eq:taper-signal}\\
 A_{ij}&=a_ik_{ij}t_j,
 \label{eq:taper-A}\\
 V_{y,i}&=a_i^2\sum_jk_{ij}^2t_j^2V_j,
 \label{eq:taper-var}\\
 W_{y,i}&={1\over a_i^2\sum_jk_{ij}^2t_j^2V_j}
 \quad\hbox{when valid}.
 \label{eq:taper-weight}
\end{align}
The powers are fixed by the signal coefficients: input taper is squared inside
the sum and output taper is squared outside.  Applying a taper once in signal
but once, twice, or with a reciprocal heuristic in weight space cannot be
formal inverse variance unless it reduces algebraically to
Equation~\eqref{eq:taper-weight}.

For template $p$,
\begin{equation}
 R_i^{(t,a)}(p)=a_i\sum_jk_{ij}t_jp_j,
 \label{eq:taper-response}
\end{equation}
and the response-corrected variance is
\begin{equation}
 \Var(\widehat\alpha_i)=
 {a_i^2\sum_jk_{ij}^2t_j^2V_j
  \over[a_i\sum_jk_{ij}t_jp_j]^2}.
 \label{eq:taper-response-var}
\end{equation}
For nonzero $a_i$, it cancels.  Deterministic output taper scales amplitude and
uncertainty together and creates no information.  Input taper changes the
relative coefficients and generally does not cancel.  Tapering, discarding
samples, locally renormalizing a truncated kernel, masking an output, and
multiplying by heuristic confidence are five distinct operations.

Until a single estimator, response product, and formal variance with the exact
powers above are specified and tested, fractional taper should remain rejected
for accepted \code{convolve} configurations.  A redesigned future mode should
store taper, response, and support independently rather than encoding them in
formal inverse variance.

\section{Response normalization and photometric interpretation}
\label{sec:response}

For $\E[x_j]=\alpha p_j+\mu_{0,j}$, define
\begin{equation}
 R_i(p)=\sum_jA_{ij}p_j.
 \label{eq:template-response}
\end{equation}
After subtracting the deterministic affine offset and any named baseline
response, a local estimator is
\begin{align}
 \widehat\alpha_i&={y_i-c_i-(A\boldsymbol\mu_0)_i\over R_i(p)},
 \label{eq:alpha-hat}\\
 \Var(\widehat\alpha_i)&={V_{y,i}\over R_i(p)^2}.
 \label{eq:alpha-var}
\end{align}
This is meaningful only when $p$, its alignment, the local baseline, and a
well-conditioned nonzero response are defined.  When the template occupies
multiple correlated output pixels, the appropriate generalized least-squares
estimator is
\begin{align}
 \widehat\alpha&={\boldsymbol r^{\mathsf T}C_y^{-1}
      (\boldsymbol y-\boldsymbol c-\boldsymbol\mu_{y,0})
      \over \boldsymbol r^{\mathsf T}C_y^{-1}\boldsymbol r},
 \label{eq:gls-alpha}\\
 \Var(\widehat\alpha)&={1\over
      \boldsymbol r^{\mathsf T}C_y^{-1}\boldsymbol r},
 \label{eq:gls-alpha-var}
\end{align}
where $\boldsymbol r=A\boldsymbol p$.  A diagonal variance plane alone is
insufficient for the exact form.

Response identities must name the template:
\begin{itemize}
\item DC normalization is $R(\boldsymbol1)$; a unit-sum interior kernel gives
one.
\item Delta peak response is the appropriate kernel coefficient, commonly
$k_0$, not $\sum k$.
\item A beam-template peak response is $(k*p)$ evaluated at the chosen center.
\item Integrated response depends on pixel solid angle, map unit convention,
and aperture or template normalization.
\item Matched-filter normalization is set by the template and noise covariance,
not by unit DC gain.
\end{itemize}
Thus \code{mJy/beam} amplitude and \code{mJy} integrated flux are not
interchangeable.  A unit-sum smoother preserves a constant field in the
interior but generally lowers the peak of a point source.

For any nonzero deterministic response correction $R$,
\begin{equation}
 {y/R\over\sqrt{V_y/R^2}}={y\over\sqrt{V_y}}
 \label{eq:snr-response-invariance}
\end{equation}
up to the sign convention for $R$.  Correcting both amplitude and uncertainty
does not improve S/N.

\section{Formal and empirical uncertainty products}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

Within the diagonal approximation, a formal inverse variance must be derived
from one named variance:
\begin{equation}
 W_i^{\rm formal}=
 \begin{cases}
 1/V_{y,i},&V_{y,i}>0\hbox{ finite and output valid},\\
 \invalid,&\hbox{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:formal-weight}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
 \sigma_i^{\rm formal}&=\sqrt{V_{y,i}},
 \label{eq:formal-sigma}\\
 Z_i^{\rm formal}&={y_i\over\sqrt{V_{y,i}}}.
 \label{eq:formal-standardized}
\end{align}
The latter is a formal standardized amplitude, not automatically an
empirically calibrated significance.

For $N\ge2$ jackknife realizations $z_i^{(n)}$ filtered with the same operator,
fixed support, fixed fill, and fixed weights,
\begin{align}
 \bar z_i&={1\over N}\sum_{n=1}^Nz_i^{(n)},
 \label{eq:jackknife-mean}\\
 s_i^2&={1\over N-1}\sum_{n=1}^N(z_i^{(n)}-\bar z_i)^2.
 \label{eq:jackknife-var}
\end{align}
This is a diagonal empirical variance conditional on the realized filtering
policy.  It does not capture coherent artifacts common to every realization,
and spatial covariance remains unrepresented.  If realization masks, weights,
support, fill, or normalization change, the $z^{(n)}$ are not samples of one
common estimator without further modeling.

A globally calibrated formal spatial pattern may be defined explicitly as
\begin{align}
 \gamma&=\operatorname{Calibrate}\!\left(
   \{s_i^2,V_{y,i}^{\rm formal}\}_{i\in\mathcal Q}\right),
 \label{eq:global-calibration}\\
 V_i^{\rm calibrated}&=\gamma V_{y,i}^{\rm formal},\qquad
 W_i^{\rm calibrated}={W_i^{\rm formal}\over\gamma}.
 \label{eq:calibrated-weight}
\end{align}
It is not pixelwise $1/s_i^2$ unless that is explicitly the chosen definition.
The product name and metadata must state which pattern is formal, which scalar
is empirical, the calibration region $\mathcal Q$, and the estimator to which
it applies.

\end{longtable}

Invalid output requires an explicit validity authority.  Zero stochastic
variance at zero support is invalid, not infinite inverse variance.  If a
response or support product is introduced later, it must be an explicit
dimensionless plane: response must identify its template, while support must
identify its eligibility mask, coefficient norm, and validity floor.

\section{Analytic limiting cases}
\label{sec:cases}

All cases below follow from the preceding equations and were written before
inspection of the candidate implementation.

\subsection{Identity, boxcar, and uniform white noise}

For $k=(1)$ and $O=T=1$, $y=x$, $V_y=V$, $W_y=w$, response and support are
one.
For an $m$-pixel boxcar $k_j=1/m$ with full support and uniform variance
$\sigma^2$,
\begin{equation}
 y={1\over m}\sum_jx_j,\quad V_y={\sigma^2\over m},\quad
 W_y=mW_{\rm in}.
 \label{eq:case-boxcar}
\end{equation}
For $m=4$, $\sum k^2=1/4$, so the output RMS is one half the input RMS and the
inverse variance is four times the input inverse variance.

\subsection{Nonuniform variance and a low-weight valid input}

For $k=(1/2,1/2)$, $V_1=1$, and $V_2=100$,
\begin{equation}
 y={x_1+x_2\over2},\qquad V_y={1\over4}(1+100)=25.25,
 \quad\sigma_y\simeq5.0249,\quad W_y\simeq0.039604.
 \label{eq:case-low-weight}
\end{equation}
The second input has formal weight $0.01$ but remains valid.  Omitting it only
from variance would report $V_y=0.25$, understate uncertainty by a factor
$\sqrt{101}\simeq10.05$, and overstate $|$S/N$|$ by the same factor while the
signal still depends equally on $x_2$.

\subsection{Constant sky and binary edge support}

Take $k=(1/2,1/2)$, true constant sky $s=10$, output window $O=1$, and
$T=(1,0)$, so $r=1/2$.  With zero fill, $y=5$, $c=0$, and the fixed-amplitude
response to the constant sky is $1/2$.  With fill $b=4$, $y=7$, $c=2$, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:constant-edge-mean} gives
$4+(10-4)/2=7$.  With correct fill $b=s=10$, $y=10$ despite partial stochastic
support.  If the retained input variance is $V_1=4$, formal variance is
$V_y=(1/2)^2 4=1$ in all three deterministic-fill cases.  The response-corrected
constant-sky estimate is $(y-c)/r=10$ with variance $1/r^2=4$.

Full support $T=(1,1)$ gives $r=1$ and the interior estimator.  One valid
contributor gives the result above.  Zero support $T=(0,0)$ gives only the fill
contribution and zero stochastic variance but must be marked invalid.

\subsection{Delta and beam-shaped sources}

For a unit delta at input $q$ and zero fill,
\begin{equation}
 y_i=O_i k_{iq}T_q,\qquad R_i(\delta_q)=O_i k_{iq}T_q.
 \label{eq:case-delta}
\end{equation}
The peak is $k_{qq}$ for a centered translation-invariant kernel, not one in
general.  Under full support $O=T=1$, a unit-amplitude beam template $p$ has
output $k*p$ and central peak response
$R_0(p)=\sum_jk_{0j}p_j$.  A beam-shaped source is
therefore broadened and its peak generally changed even though DC response is
one.  Integrated-flux preservation additionally depends on map units, pixel
solid angle, beam convention, and aperture; it does not follow from the peak
or DC response alone.

\subsection{Fractional tapers}

With $k=(1/2,1/2)$, $t=(1,1/2)$, $a=1$, and equal variance $V=4$,
\begin{equation}
 y={1\over2}x_1+{1\over4}x_2,\qquad
 V_y=4\left({1\over4}+{1\over16}\right)=1.25.
 \label{eq:case-input-taper}
\end{equation}
An output taper $a=1/2$ changes this to $y/2$ and $V_y/4=0.3125$; formal
inverse variance rises from $0.8$ to $3.2$ solely because the stored amplitude
unit scale changed.  The response-corrected estimator and its information are
unchanged because the output taper cancels as in
Equation~\eqref{eq:taper-response-var}.  Applying both tapers uses both stated
powers; it is not a sample-count correction.

\subsection{Support with nonnegative and signed kernels}

For output window $O=1$, nonnegative $k=(1/4,1/2,1/4)$, and $T=(1,1,0)$,
$r=q^{(|k|)}=3/4$, while $q^{(k^2)}=(1/16+1/4)/(3/8)=5/6$.
They answer different questions.  For signed $k=(-1,1,1)$ normalized to sum
one and $T=(1,1,0)$, the signed mask convolution is zero even though two thirds
of the footprint and two thirds of the absolute operator mass are present:
$q^{(|k|)}=2/3$.  Signed response zero is real for a constant template, but it
is not zero geometric support.

\subsection{Central empirical $N=2$ and rejection at $N=1$}

For filtered realizations $z^{(1)}=2$ and $z^{(2)}=6$, $\bar z=4$ and
\begin{equation}
 s^2={(-2)^2+2^2\over2-1}=8.
 \label{eq:case-N2}
\end{equation}
Any stable online central-moment accumulation must return exactly this value
within arithmetic precision.  At $N=1$, the mean-subtracted central sample
variance in Equation~\eqref{eq:jackknife-var} is undefined; any product that
requests that estimator must fail closed rather than emit zero, infinity, or
a formal substitute under an empirical label.  The separately named
known-zero-mean second-moment estimator remains defined for one realization.

\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Exact shared signal and edge operator}

Both baseline and candidate use an FFT convolution, hence a periodic/circular
discrete operator $K$ on the finite map.  The filter template is required to
have a sufficiently conditioned nonzero sum and is divided by that sum.  Let
$h$ denote this globally unit-sum kernel.  The edge science region is selected
from positive formal weight above the \code{cov_cut}-derived threshold and,
when available, positive coverage above a fraction of its core median.  The
region is non-periodically dilated by a Euclidean disk clipped at the map
boundary, with radius
\begin{equation}
 d=\max\{1,\lceil f_{\rm guard}\max({\rm FWHM}_{\rm pix},1)\rceil\}
 \label{eq:implemented-guard-radius}
\end{equation}
to create the accepted binary window $T$.  Thus a pixel below \code{cov_cut}
may still have $T=1$ because it lies in the guardband.

The exterior fill is not a configured constant.  It is
\begin{equation}
 b(\boldsymbol x)=\operatorname{median}\{x_j:j\hbox{ is finite and in the
 science region}\},
 \label{eq:implemented-background}
\end{equation}
with zero as the empty-set fallback.  The exact stored signal is
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol y=D_TK\{D_T\boldsymbol x+(I-D_T)b(\boldsymbol x)\boldsymbol1\},
 \label{eq:implemented-signal}
\end{equation}
where $D_T=\diag(T)$.  The final $D_T$ is an output mask applied after
convolution.  The mapmaking kernel image is pre- and post-multiplied in the
same manner, but it is a data product being filtered, not the convolution
template $h$ itself.

Equation~\eqref{eq:implemented-signal} is nonlinear because the median and the
weight/coverage-derived window depend on realized data products.  Conditional
on the realized $T$ and $b$, its stochastic signal operator is
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rm cond}=D_TKD_T,
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol c_{\rm cond}=D_TK(I-D_T)b\boldsymbol1.
 \label{eq:implemented-conditional-operator}
\end{equation}
The candidate formal calculation treats both as fixed.  It therefore omits
the fill covariance and cross-covariance in
Equation~\eqref{eq:random-fill-cov}, and also conditions on the realized
window.  This may be a defensible approximation after measurement, but it is
not an unconditional formal covariance and is currently undocumented and
unvalidated.

Noise realizations use
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol z^{(n)}_{\rm out}=D_TKD_T\boldsymbol z^{(n)}_{\rm in}.
 \label{eq:implemented-noise-operator}
\end{equation}
They reuse science-derived $T$ and do not re-estimate or add $b$.  They
therefore sample the conditional stochastic operator in
Equation~\eqref{eq:implemented-conditional-operator}, but not median-fill
uncertainty, realization-dependent masks, or artifacts common to all
jackknives.

\subsection{Baseline mainline}

Define $M_j=1$ only where the post-edge input weight is finite, positive, and
at least the \code{cov_cut}-derived threshold, and zero otherwise.  Baseline
forms two circular convolutions with $h^2$ and reports, on output-window
support,
\begin{align}
 \widetilde V_i&=\sum_j h_{ij}^2M_jV_j,
 \label{eq:baseline-var-numerator}\\
 m_i&=\sum_j h_{ij}^2M_j,
 \label{eq:baseline-k2-mask}\\
 W^{\rm base}_i&={m_i\over\widetilde V_i}\,T_i^2.
 \label{eq:baseline-weight}
\end{align}
Its implied variance is Equation~\eqref{eq:k2-mask-ratio}.  The signal in
Equation~\eqref{eq:implemented-signal} is divided by neither $m_i$ nor
$\sqrt{m_i}$, and guardband inputs below \code{cov_cut} still enter signal.
Baseline therefore has two definite mathematical defects: the $k^2$-support
division has no matching stored signal estimator, and a low positive-weight
input can enter signal while being omitted from formal variance.  In a
uniform, fully supported interior, the first defect cancels the expected
variance reduction and leaves formal inverse variance equal to its input
value instead of Equation~\eqref{eq:white-weight-ratio}.

\code{cov_cut} consequently has several distinct roles: it seeds the edge
science mask; it defines the final displayed \code{coverage_bool_I} from the
current output weight; it selects pixels for empirical global calibration;
and, only in baseline and Candidate~1, it incorrectly removes variance terms
without changing the signal.  It is not a single scientific support
definition.  The exact C++ display-mask predicate is the negated strict
comparison $\neg(W_i<\tau_{m cov})$, not a finite-positive validity test;
in particular, a NaN comparison is false and therefore produces mask value
one.

\subsection{Candidate 1: \texttt{fa8f8a5b9}}

Candidate~1 removes the division by $m_i$ and intends to implement
Equation~\eqref{eq:edge-var}, adds a relative validity floor, requires
$N\ge2$ for central empirical variance, applies the $N/(N-1)$ correction to
its accumulated central moment, and changes the point-source compatibility
metadata.  However, it retains $M_j$'s \code{cov_cut} criterion while the
signal retains all positive-weight inputs allowed by $T$.  It therefore
implements
\begin{equation}
 V_i^{(1)}=T_i^2\sum_jh_{ij}^2M_jV_j,
 \label{eq:candidate1-var}
\end{equation}
which remains a signal/variance operator mismatch.  Its difference-of-moments
accumulation is also unnecessarily vulnerable to cancellation for large
offsets.  The commit is a useful but internally incomplete correction.

\subsection{Candidate successor and combined stack}

Commit \code{02a198cbf} removes \code{cov_cut} from the input variance mask,
uses every finite positive input weight that the binary window passes, and
replaces difference-of-moments accumulation with Welford's stable central
moment.  For the accepted binary mode it implements
\begin{align}
 V_i^{\rm cand}&=T_i^2\sum_{j:0<w_j<\infty}h_{ij}^2T_jV_j,
 \label{eq:candidate-var}\\
 q_i^{\rm valid}&={\sum_{j:0<w_j<\infty}h_{ij}^2T_j\over\sum_jh_{ij}^2},
 \label{eq:candidate-valid-support}\\
 W_i^{\rm cand}&=
 \begin{cases}
 1/V_i^{\rm cand},&q_i^{\rm valid}>10^{-6},\ V_i^{\rm cand}>0
                    \hbox{ finite},\ T_i=1,\\
 0,&\hbox{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:candidate-weight}
\end{align}
The floor is a dimensionless fraction of total $h^2$ mass and remains valid
for signed kernels.  It controls only whether the formal output weight is
zero; it neither renormalizes signal nor variance and is not persisted as a
support plane.  Conditional on $T$ and $b$, and provided every zero or
non-finite-weight input carries no unmodelled stochastic contribution,
Equations~\eqref{eq:candidate-var} and \eqref{eq:candidate-weight} are the
correct diagonal variance and inverse variance of the stored amplitude in
Equation~\eqref{eq:implemented-signal}.  Normal application
\code{MapBuffer::normalize_maps} preparation zeros signal and every noise
realization where effective weight is zero; lower-level callers must preserve
that precondition.  The unresolved data-derived-fill covariance remains.

The successor also rejects the dormant cosine taper for accepted
\code{convolve} and Wiener \code{lowpass_only}; fixes output-policy routing so
the effective typed configuration, rather than a product-output side effect,
controls empirical normalization; records \code{NNOISE},
\code{VAREST=central_sample_variance}, \code{VARDDOF=1}, and
\code{MEANSUB=true}; and makes convolve point-source aliases
\code{TYPE=convolved_amplitude} without \code{RESPNORM}.  It tests exact
$N=2$, mean-subtracted $N=1$ rejection, and large-offset stability.  The
known-zero-mean second-moment path remains a separate estimator.

The load-time guard uses producer metadata rather than the consumer run's
current filter configuration.  Historical filtered products missing both
\code{FILTEROP} and explicit \code{FLFBACK} approval fail closed because their
producer operator cannot be established from the signal HDU.  This is a
restart/product-provenance guard, not a new response or support estimator.

The serial and OpenMP implementations follow the same intended sequence.
Existing focused and regression gates establish code-path consistency, not
scientific acceptance.  In particular, they do not establish median-fill

\subsection{Dormant fractional taper}

The dormant path replaces $x$ by $b+(x-b)t$, multiplies input weight by
$t^2$, convolves, and then multiplies output signal by $a$ and output weight
by $a^2$.  Formal propagation begins from the already tapered weight, hence
uses $1/(w t^2)=V/t^2$ inside the $h^2$ convolution, followed by multiplication
of inverse variance by $a^2$.  These are the reciprocal of the required input
power and the wrong output power for Equation~\eqref{eq:taper-var}.  It is a
definite signal/variance mismatch for stored tapered amplitude.  Rejecting
this path for the accepted modes is correct; redesign would require a new
explicit estimator rather than a small weight-space patch.

\subsection{Empirical and calibrated products}

For central empirical products the successor computes
Equation~\eqref{eq:jackknife-var} with Welford accumulation.  On calibration
pixels $\mathcal Q$ selected by positive formal weight, positive empirical
variance, and the \code{cov_cut} threshold, it then computes
\begin{align}
 \rho&=\operatorname{median}_{i\in\mathcal Q}
        \left(W^{\rm formal}_i s_i^2\right),
 \label{eq:implemented-median-ratio}\\
 g&={1\over\rho},
 \label{eq:implemented-emp-scale}\\
 W_i^{\rm empirical}&=gW_i^{\rm formal}.
 \label{eq:implemented-emp-weight}
\end{align}
When empirical normalization is enabled, \code{weight_I} is replaced by
Equation~\eqref{eq:implemented-emp-weight}.  It is a formal spatial pattern
with one empirical scalar, not pixelwise $1/s_i^2$, and generally
\begin{equation}
 W_i^{\rm empirical}\ne{1\over s_i^2}.
 \label{eq:empirical-weight-not-pixelwise}
\end{equation}
Here $W^{\rm formal}$ names the snapshot entering this filtered empirical
calibration stage.  It is not necessarily a pristine mapmaker weight: the
snapshot can inherit an upstream scalar empirical calibration.  Its metadata
must therefore identify both the propagated estimator and the input-weight
provenance.
The two empirical ratios written by the candidate are consequently
\begin{align}
 Z_i^{\rm pixel}&=y_i\sqrt{W_i^{\rm empirical}},
 \label{eq:implemented-pixel-snr}\\
 \sigma_i^{\rm direct}&=\sqrt{s_i^2},
 \label{eq:implemented-direct-sigma}\\
 Z_i^{\rm direct}&=
 \begin{cases}y_i/\sigma_i^{\rm direct},&s_i^2>0,\\0,&\hbox{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:implemented-direct-snr}
\end{align}
They are different estimators: the first retains the formal spatial pattern
and a robust global scale, while the second uses each pixel's direct sample
variance.  Neither represents a response-corrected point-source flux.

\subsection{Defect classification and verdict}

The audit classifications are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Definite mathematical error:} baseline divides propagated
variance by local $h^2$ support without applying the corresponding
square-root normalization to signal
(Equation~\eqref{eq:baseline-weight}).
\item \textbf{Signal/variance operator mismatch:} baseline and Candidate~1
omit below-\code{cov_cut} variance contributions that remain in signal.
\item \textbf{Signal/variance operator mismatch:} dormant fractional taper
uses reciprocal input-taper variance powers and the wrong output-weight power.
\item \textbf{Product naming/metadata error:} baseline labels unnormalized
convolved-amplitude aliases as point-source products and claims
\code{RESPNORM=1}; the successor corrects this.
\item \textbf{Diagonal-covariance approximation:} every variance plane omits
Equation~\eqref{eq:affine-output-cov}; no output covariance product exists.
\item \textbf{Unresolved approximation:} $b$ is a nonlinear same-map median,
but its variance and cross-covariance are absent; $T$ is also treated as fixed.
\item \textbf{Inverse-variance versus support risk:} the $h^2$ validity floor,
edge window, and \code{coverage_bool_I} are distinct policies, but no explicit
edge-support or response plane is persisted.
\item \textbf{Scientifically defensible convention:} fixed globally
normalized amplitude, conditional diagonal propagation, binary masking, and
global empirical calibration are coherent when named honestly and validated.
\end{enumerate}

The two-commit stack should be \textbf{amended}: retain its numerical core as
one coherent conditional stored-amplitude correction, with the successor
superseding Candidate~1's low-weight and accumulation details, but amend the
contract, metadata, support/response product decision, and validation evidence
before integration.  The commits need not be split on scientific grounds;
neither should be integrated unchanged, and no integration is performed here.

\section{Recommended Citlali contract}
\label{sec:recommendation}

The recommended estimator is the fixed affine operator in
Equations~\eqref{eq:edge-stored}--\eqref{eq:edge-cov}.  Any future response or
support products must remain distinct; the bounded repair withholds filtered
production selection while their identities are unresolved.  Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Normalize a scientifically admissible smoothing kernel globally and
record its normalization and sign/conditioning policy.
\item Apply the realized binary input and output windows consistently to
signal, formal variance, response, empirical realizations, and any future
support product.  A valid low-weight input remains in both signal and
variance.  A zero or non-finite-weight input is conditioned on as carrying no
stochastic contribution; normal application preparation enforces this by
zeroing its signal and noise, and lower-level callers must do the same.
\item Treat background fill as a named deterministic affine contribution.  If
it is data-derived, either propagate Equation~\eqref{eq:random-fill-cov} or
state and validate the omitted uncertainty approximation.
\item Store the fixed filtered amplitude, its formal variance/inverse variance,
the $N-1$ empirical variance when $N\ge2$, and any calibrated weight with
truthful estimator metadata.
\item Do not claim or store a response without a named template.  Do not
promote the internal $h^2$ numerical-overlap guard, the edge window, or
\code{coverage_bool_I} to scientific support.  Before production selection,
choose and validate a dimensionless support equation, eligibility mask, and
floor; until then mark unit-sum-convolved products as selection-unready.
\item Retain historical point-source extension names only as explicitly marked
\code{convolved_amplitude} compatibility aliases.  Add actual photometric
products only with a named beam/template, response correction, units, and
validation.
\item Continue rejecting fractional taper in accepted \code{convolve} and
Wiener \code{lowpass_only} configurations until it is redesigned around
Equations~\eqref{eq:taper-signal}--\eqref{eq:taper-response-var}.
\end{enumerate}

This chooses fixed behavior over silent local normalization.  A future local
estimator may be scientifically useful, but it must be a separate named mode
implementing both Equations~\eqref{eq:local-signal} and
\eqref{eq:local-var}, with response and conditioning gates.

The review must still decide (i) whether the same-map median-fill covariance
is propagated, bounded as negligible, or replaced by a deterministic policy;
(ii) the scientific template and normalization of any response plane;
(iii) whether $q^{(|k|)}$, $q^{(k^2)}$, or both are exposed and what evidence
sets their validity floors; (iv) whether empirical calibration remains a
single robust scale or becomes a modeled spatial correction; (v) the intended
photometric interpretation of \code{mJy/beam} after smoothing; and (vi) how
filtered covariance is represented or supplied to downstream multi-pixel
analyses.  Those are product/science decisions, not implementation details.

\section{Consequences for fruit-loop feedback}
\label{sec:fruitloop}

Pending an approved support/response contract and external validation, the
frozen fruit-loop spatial estimator must remain fail-closed.  No filtered
product may currently select feedback support or supply feedback amplitude,
weight, or gain.  The inspected draft consumer correctly proposes raw
coadd amplitude for feedback and uses filtered maps only to select spatial
support, but it currently relies on \code{coverage_bool_I}, which is a
final-weight threshold mask rather than an edge-response or absolute-support
plane.  Under the recommended contract:
\begin{itemize}
\item Future credible support may use an explicit validity mask plus an
approved explicit support plane; neither is amplitude or inverse variance.
\item Local thresholding may use an empirically calibrated stored-amplitude
S/N only where its operator, support, fill, and covariance limitations are
accepted.  Formal-standardized amplitude is not a substitute under an
empirical S/N label.
\item Morphological selection may consume a threshold mask but must calibrate
connected-component sizes and dilation under the filter-induced covariance of
Equation~\eqref{eq:affine-output-cov}.
\item Feedback weighting and gain are separate policy quantities.  Neither
formal inverse variance nor support automatically defines a stable feedback
gain.
\item The amplitude projected into the timestream should remain the explicitly
chosen raw coadd amplitude unless a separately reviewed filtered/response-
corrected amplitude contract is adopted.  Detection support must not silently
replace feedback amplitude.
\end{itemize}
Only after the required validation and approval of an explicit support and
response policy could \code{sig2noise_point_source_I}---despite its legacy
extension name---be evaluated for local thresholding, because Candidate~2
truthfully identifies it as
\code{TYPE=convolved_amplitude} and it uses the direct pixelwise jackknife
variance in Equation~\eqref{eq:implemented-direct-snr}.  It must be
intersected with raw-map validity and a new explicit response/support policy.
\code{sig2noise_pixel_I} is a useful secondary diagnostic of the globally
calibrated formal spatial pattern, not an interchangeable estimate.
\code{weight_I}, \code{weight_formal_I}, \code{coverage_bool_I}, and the
filtered amplitude aliases are not presently safe as confidence, gain, or
direct feedback amplitude.  Therefore no filtered product is yet safe for
production fruit-loop support selection.
Correlated filtered pixels reduce the number of independent threshold trials,
inflate connected regions relative to independent-pixel intuition, couple
dilation to the kernel scale, and can create edge-adjacent false support where
response or fill bias is not guarded.  Blank controls and injections must
calibrate these effects before filtered products select production feedback.

amplitudes and edge distances in all arrays.  Measure stored filtered amplitude
against the exact applied operator, any proposed response-corrected amplitude,
uncertainty coverage, S/N calibration, centroid/morphology bias, and support
selection completeness/false discovery.  Pre-register acceptable deviations
from analytic operator predictions and derive empirical tolerances from blank
controls and repeated injections.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The mathematical authority is the estimator and its full covariance.  In the
diagonal approximation, formal variance is obtained only by squaring the exact
signal coefficients.  Response correction, support, and confidence are
separate deterministic or policy transformations and cannot be folded into
inverse variance without changing its meaning.  The implementation verdict is
completed in Section~\ref{sec:implementation} after independent derivation.
