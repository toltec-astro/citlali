\begin{abstract}
This document freezes a source-independent scientific contract for ordinary
naive mapmaking and observation coaddition. Conditional on fixed calibration,
pointing, eligibility, and processed-timestream covariance inputs, both stages
are linear estimators. Their full covariance is obtained by propagating the
same linear operators used for the signal. A normalized gridding denominator
is a normalization product; it equals statistical inverse variance only under
explicit additional conditions. Observation mapmaking and coaddition can be
reviewed coherently as one package because the coadd is a second linear
combination of the same map-product identity, response, covariance, support,
and validity objects. This draft does not claim implementation conformity,
validation completion, scientific-owner approval, or new production authority.
\end{abstract}

\section{Package boundary and coherence decision}

The package includes the shared map-product identity; ordinary naive
sample-to-pixel accumulation; pre-normalized and normalized signal and
response/kernel products; formal covariance and any diagonal weight claim;
hits, exposure coverage, support, and validity; observation-map identity;
observation coaddition; and the output contract presented to downstream
packages. Noise realizations enter only to test whether they traverse the same
fixed map and coadd operators.

The package excludes RTC/PTC internals, final calibration/astrometric/flagging
policy, the construction and empirical calibration of noise realizations,
JINC internal mathematics, maximum-likelihood production implementation,
post-map filters, source fitting, mode-specific inference, Beammap inference,
and fruit-loop feedback mathematics.

No split is required by the independent mathematics. For fixed upstream state,
an observation map is \(\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_o=A_o\boldsymbol y_o\) and a
coadd is \(\widehat{\boldsymbol s}=B\boldsymbol z\), where
\(\boldsymbol z=J\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{\rm stack}\) is the preselected
finite-valid observation-map stack. Consequently the complete selected-space
operator is \(BJA_{\rm block}\), with \(A_{\rm block}\) the block-diagonal
stack of observation operators; its covariance is propagated through the same
operator, and its response, units, map identity, support, and validity cannot
be reviewed coherently without the observation-map contract. The two stages
remain separately identifiable within this one package and can receive
separate findings or repair gates.

\section{General linear and generalized least-squares estimator}

Let \(S_o\) be a row selector that retains exactly the entries allowed by
\(M_o\). Define
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol y_o &= S_o\boldsymbol d_o,
  &X_o &= S_oH_o,
  &N_o &= S_o C_{d,o}S_o^\trans .
  \label{eq:selected-system}
\end{align}
Here
\(C_{d,o}=\Cov(\boldsymbol d_o-\E[\boldsymbol d_o\mid
\boldsymbol m_o,\theta]\mid\boldsymbol m_o,\theta)\), and therefore
\(N_o=\Cov(\boldsymbol y_o-\E[\boldsymbol y_o\mid
\boldsymbol m_o,\theta]\mid\boldsymbol m_o,\theta)\). These are noise/error
covariances, not ensemble covariances of a random sky plus noise.
Selection occurs before covariance inversion; with correlated noise, merely
multiplying a full inverse covariance by a diagonal mask is not generally the
precision of the retained samples.

Every displayed \(N_o^{-1}\) requires \(N_o\) to be positive definite on the
selected stochastic subspace. If fixed preprocessing leaves deterministic
null modes, the system must first be expressed in a basis of that subspace (or
the null modes explicitly quotiented out) and covariance inverted there. The
outer Moore--Penrose inverse of a normal matrix does not make an undefined
inner covariance inverse valid.

For a fixed positive-definite analysis precision \(Q_o\), the generalized
linear estimator is
\begin{align}
  F_o &= X_o^\trans Q_o X_o,
  \label{eq:normal-matrix}\\
  \widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{o,Q}
    &= F_o^{+}X_o^\trans Q_o\boldsymbol y_o
     \equiv A_{o,Q}\boldsymbol y_o,
  \label{eq:general-linear-estimator}
\end{align}
where \({}^{+}\) is the Moore--Penrose inverse on the explicitly identifiable
subspace. Unconstrained null modes are invalid; a pseudoinverse does not turn
them into measured sky.

The conditional expectation, response, and full covariance are
\begin{align}
  \E[\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{o,Q}\mid\theta]
    &= A_{o,Q}\E[\boldsymbol y_o\mid\theta],
  \label{eq:general-mean}\\
  R_{o,Q} &= A_{o,Q}X_o,
  \label{eq:general-response}\\
  C_{m,o,Q}
    &= A_{o,Q}N_oA_{o,Q}^\trans .
  \label{eq:general-covariance}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:general-covariance}, not a diagonal product name, is the
mathematical noise propagation conditional on fixed \(\boldsymbol m_o\) and
\(\theta\). If instead a random sky is marginalized with covariance
\(C_{\rm sky}\), the output ensemble covariance additionally contains
\(R_{o,Q}C_{\rm sky}R_{o,Q}^\trans\), plus sky--noise cross terms when they are
correlated. When \(Q_o=N_o^{-1}\),
the covariance model is correct, and the supported columns of \(X_o\) are
identifiable, the GLS estimator is
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{o,\mathrm{GLS}}
    &= (X_o^\trans N_o^{-1}X_o)^{+}
       X_o^\trans N_o^{-1}\boldsymbol y_o,
  \label{eq:gls-estimator}\\
  C_{m,o,\mathrm{GLS}}
    &= (X_o^\trans N_o^{-1}X_o)^{+}
  \label{eq:gls-covariance}
\end{align}
on that subspace. If \(Q_o\neq N_o^{-1}\), the correct covariance is the
sandwich in Equation~\eqref{eq:general-covariance}; \(F_o\) is then an analysis
normalization, not statistical precision.

If upstream preprocessing is a fixed linear operator \(L\), then
\(C_d=L C_{\rm raw}L^\trans\). Temporal correlation, shared detector modes, and
preprocessing-induced covariance enter there and propagate through
Equation~\eqref{eq:general-covariance}.

Calibration, astrometric, or response parameters \(\boldsymbol\eta\) add, to
first order, a systematic term
\begin{equation}
 C_{m,\eta}=J_\eta C_\eta J_\eta^\trans,
 \qquad
 J_\eta=\left.
 \frac{\partial\E[\widehat{\boldsymbol m}]}{\partial\boldsymbol\eta}
 \right|_{\widehat{\boldsymbol\eta}},
 \label{eq:systematic-covariance}
\end{equation}
plus cross terms when parameter errors and sample noise are correlated. This
is distinct from the conditional formal covariance.

\section{Ordinary naive normalized gridding}

For one observation and map group, retain only eligible finite samples, write
\(\boldsymbol k_o\) for the corresponding selected entries of the abstract
sample-domain kernel tracer, and require the ordinary gridding coefficients
\(G_o\) to be dimensionless and nonnegative. Then
let \(\Omega_o=\diag(\omega_{oi})\) contain finite nonnegative gridding
weights. Define the pre-normalized signal numerator and gridding normalization
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol n_o &= G_o\Omega_o\boldsymbol y_o,
  \label{eq:naive-numerator}\\
  \boldsymbol q_o &= G_o\Omega_o\boldsymbol 1,
  \qquad D_o=\diag(\boldsymbol q_o).
  \label{eq:naive-denominator}
\end{align}
The supported-pixel pseudoinverse \(D_o^{+}\) is defined as \(1/q_{op}\) only
where the declared validity rule accepts finite \(q_{op}>0\); it is zero in
the algebraic operator for unsupported pixels, whose persisted signal is
nevertheless marked invalid rather than interpreted as zero sky. The ordinary
naive map and its exact fixed-state operator are
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol m}_o
    &=D_o^{+}\boldsymbol n_o
      =D_o^{+}G_o\Omega_o\boldsymbol y_o
      \equiv A_o\boldsymbol y_o.
  \label{eq:naive-map}
\end{align}

For the response/kernel tracer,
\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol n_{k,o} &= G_o\Omega_o\boldsymbol k_o,
  \label{eq:kernel-numerator}\\
  \widehat{\boldsymbol k}_{o} &=D_o^{+}\boldsymbol n_{k,o}.
  \label{eq:normalized-kernel}
\end{align}
Signal and kernel must use identical eligibility, projection, gridding weights,
normalization, grouping, and boundary decisions for a shared map operator. A
kernel with units \(U_g\) is a named response/template product, not a weight or
probability. Its source position, amplitude normalization, array/group, WCS,
iteration, and operator identity are part of the contract.

When the stored kernel is claimed to be the realized response to a declared
unit-bearing map template \(\boldsymbol\tau\), the required identity is
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol k_o=X_o\boldsymbol\tau,
 \qquad
 \widehat{\boldsymbol k}_o
 =A_oX_o\boldsymbol\tau
 =R_o\boldsymbol\tau.
 \label{eq:kernel-response-identity}
\end{equation}
The response matrix \(R_o\) is dimensionless while the realized kernel image
has the signal unit carried by \(\boldsymbol\tau\). If
Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel-response-identity} is not established, the stored
quantity is only a kernel tracer and must not be advertised as the realized
map response.

\subsection{Relation to diagonal weighted least squares}

Suppose samples obey
\(\boldsymbol y=X\boldsymbol m+\boldsymbol\epsilon\) with independent
\(\Var(\epsilon_i)=\sigma_i^2\), let
\(\omega_i=\sigma_i^{-2}\), and approximate the normal matrix by its diagonal.
Then
\begin{align}
  f_p &=\sum_i X_{ip}^2\omega_i,
  \label{eq:diagonal-normalization}\\
  \widehat m_{p,\mathrm{diag\,WLS}}
    &=\frac{\sum_i X_{ip}\omega_i y_i}
            {\sum_i X_{ip}^2\omega_i}.
  \label{eq:diagonal-wls}
\end{align}
If \(G_{pi}=X_{ip}\) is one-hot, so \(G_{pi}\in\{0,1\}\) and each accepted
sample belongs to one pixel, Equations~\eqref{eq:naive-map} and
\eqref{eq:diagonal-wls} coincide. For fractional projection,
\(G_{pi}^2\neq G_{pi}\) in general: normalized gridding and diagonal WLS are
different estimators. The implementation must name which projection and
denominator it realizes; neither is silently redefined to match a stored field
name.

\section{Formal covariance, weight, and cross-pixel correlation}

The exact conditional covariance of the naive map is
\begin{equation}
 C_{m,o}=D_o^{+}G_o\Omega_o N_o\Omega_o
 G_o^\trans D_o^{+\trans}.
 \label{eq:naive-full-covariance}
\end{equation}
For independent samples and true inverse-variance weights,
\(N_o=\diag(\sigma_i^2)\) and \(\omega_i=\sigma_i^{-2}\), this becomes
\begin{align}
 \Var(\widehat m_{op}\mid\theta)
 &=\frac{\sum_iG_{opi}^2\omega_i}
         {\left(\sum_iG_{opi}\omega_i\right)^2},
 \label{eq:fractional-map-variance}\\
 \Cov(\widehat m_{op},\widehat m_{or}\mid\theta)
 &=\frac{\sum_iG_{opi}G_{ori}\omega_i}
         {q_{op}q_{or}}.
 \label{eq:fractional-cross-pixel-covariance}
\end{align}
The conditional diagonal formal inverse variance is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \phi_{op}=\frac{q_{op}^2}{\sum_iG_{opi}^2\omega_i},
 \label{eq:formal-diagonal-weight}
\end{equation}
where the denominator is finite and positive. The exact algebraic condition
for \(\phi_{op}=q_{op}\) is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\omega_i\left(G_{opi}^2-G_{opi}\right)=0.
 \label{eq:normalization-equals-weight-condition}
\end{equation}
For \(0\leq G_{opi}\leq1\) and strictly positive weights, this requires every
coefficient contributing to pixel \(p\) to be zero or one; ordinary one-hot
hard assignment is the standard sufficient guarantee. With correlated sample noise,
shared detector noise, fractional projection, or preprocessing-induced
correlations, Equation~\eqref{eq:naive-full-covariance} remains authoritative;
the map precision is not obtained by merely summing per-sample weights.

Accordingly:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(q_p\) is always a gridding normalization under this contract;
\item \(q_p\) may be called formal inverse variance only after
Equation~\eqref{eq:normalization-equals-weight-condition}, independent sample
noise, and true inverse-variance sample weights are established;
\item \(\phi_p=1/[C_m]_{pp}\) is a diagonal inverse variance but does not encode
the off-diagonal covariance; and
\item the full precision \(C_m^{+}\), when identifiable, is a matrix and is not
interchangeable with \(\phi\).
\end{itemize}

A deterministic counterexample is a single map pixel receiving
\(G_{p1}=G_{p2}=1/2\) with \(\omega_1=\omega_2=1\):
\(q_p=1\), but \(\Var(\widehat m_p)=1/2\) and \(\phi_p=2\).

\section{Response and analytic input classes}

Let the conditional selected-sample expectation for a sky map be
\(\E[\boldsymbol y]=X\boldsymbol m+\boldsymbol\mu_0\). The naive map response
matrix is
\begin{align}
 R_o&=A_oX_o,
 \label{eq:naive-response}\\
 \boldsymbol b_{m,o}&=A_o\boldsymbol\mu_{0,o},
 \qquad
 \E[\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_o]=
 R_o\boldsymbol m_o+\boldsymbol b_{m,o},
 \label{eq:map-affine-mean}\\
 \frac{\partial\E[\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_o]}{\partial\alpha}
 &=R_o\boldsymbol\tau
 \quad\hbox{for}\quad
 \boldsymbol m=\alpha\boldsymbol\tau+\boldsymbol m_0.
 \label{eq:template-response}
\end{align}
This single expression yields the required cases:

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
\item[Constant sky.]
Because numerator and denominator use the same coefficients,
\(A\boldsymbol1_{\rm sample}=\boldsymbol1_{\rm map}\) on supported pixels:
the gridder preserves a constant selected-sample vector. Preservation of a
constant sky is the stronger condition
\(R\boldsymbol1_{\rm sky}=\boldsymbol1_{\rm map}\), after any additive
\(\boldsymbol\mu_0\) is removed, and also requires the forward response to map
that sky mode to the required sample-domain constant. Neither condition alone
establishes integrated-source or point-source normalization.
\item[Delta or basis-pixel input.]
For \(\boldsymbol\tau=\boldsymbol e_{p_0}\), the column
\(R_{:p_0}\) is the complete pixelized impulse response, including projection,
preprocessing, beam, and boundary truncation.
\item[Beam/template source.]
For a declared template \(\boldsymbol\tau_b\), the recovered response is
\(R\boldsymbol\tau_b\). Peak amplitude, integrated amplitude, and fitted
template amplitude are distinct normalizations and require distinct scalar
functionals of this response.
\item[Resolved and extended modes.]
Apply Equation~\eqref{eq:naive-response} to disks, gradients, Fourier modes, or
other extended templates. A constant-sky pass does not prove preservation of
nonconstant extended structure, especially after upstream preprocessing.
\item[Variable coverage.]
For fixed selection and weights, normalization can preserve a constant input
while the covariance and response to nonconstant templates vary spatially. If
selection or weights respond to signal, the fixed-operator result is only
conditional and selection bias must be measured.
\item[Boundaries.]
Truncated projection support changes \(R\), hits, exposure, and covariance.
Local normalization can preserve a constant field at an edge while changing a
compact-source peak or integrated response. Out-of-grid samples do not wrap or
clamp unless that behavior is explicitly part of the astrometric contract.
\end{description}

A scalar transfer function is justified only after shift invariance is
demonstrated. Otherwise the response matrix or a position-dependent kernel is
required. Iteration-to-iteration response is not intrinsic to the one-pass
mapmaker. Changing only \(\boldsymbol d\) changes the mapped input, not the map

\section{Hits, exposure coverage, support, and validity}

Let \(\mathcal I_o\) be the set of entries selected by \(S_o\) after flags and
finite-value checks. The following sums iterate that set directly; they do not
attempt masking through multiplication. These products are intentionally
different:
\begin{align}
 h^{\rm geom}_{op}&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_o}\mathbf 1[G_{opi}\neq0],
 \label{eq:hits}\\
 h^{\rm wt}_{op}&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_o}
 \mathbf 1[G_{opi}\neq0\ \land\ \omega_{oi}>0],
 \label{eq:weighted-hits}\\
 x_{op}&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_o}G^{(x)}_{opi}\Delta t_{oi},
 \qquad [G^{(x)}_{opi}]=1,\quad G^{(x)}_{opi}\geq0,
 \quad\Delta t_{oi}\geq0,
 \label{eq:coverage}\\
 s_{op}&=\mathbf 1[\hbox{all declared support criteria pass}],
 \label{eq:support}\\
 v_{op}&=\mathbf 1[s_{op}=1,\ \widehat m_{op}\hbox{ and required companion
 products satisfy their finite/validity rules}].
 \label{eq:validity}
\end{align}
Both hit quantities are dimensionless. Geometric hits count eligible projected
incidences; positive-weight hits count actual weighted contributions. A stored
\code{hits} product must state which definition it realizes and whether a
fractionally projected sample counts once for every touched pixel. Exposure
coverage is seconds and uses a named exposure projection \(G^{(x)}\), which
need not equal the statistical gridding coefficients. Summing sample duration
over detectors produces detector-seconds, not ordinary elapsed seconds, unless
the definition explicitly normalizes detector multiplicity. Support is a
Boolean policy result, potentially including positive normalization, positive
formal weight, coverage threshold, geometry, or configured coverage cut.
Validity is the authority for consumer use. None is a confidence level or
response calibration.

All samples must be screened for flags, detector eligibility, finite signal,
finite projection, finite exposure, and finite weight before multiply-add
operations. Multiplication of NaN by zero is not masking. Under the
normalized-gridding contract:
\begin{itemize}
\item a finite zero weight makes no signal or normalization contribution and
does not create statistical information; it may increment geometric hits but
must not increment positive-weight hits;
\item a low positive weight follows an explicit upstream threshold/policy and
is not silently promoted or rejected by the accumulator;
\item a negative inverse-variance or ordinary nonnegative-gridding weight is
invalid; a signed-kernel estimator would require a separately approved
normalization and covariance;
\item non-finite signal, weight, projection, or exposure is ineligible and is
diagnosed according to SCI-VAL-001;
\item an out-of-bounds projection makes no contribution and must not corrupt a
neighboring pixel; and
\item zero or invalid normalization produces weight zero, hits/coverage as
actually accumulated, support/validity false, and a contract-permitted
non-finite signal/kernel representation. It is never interpreted as measured
zero sky.
\end{itemize}

\section{Units and normalization}

For the dimensionless \(G_o\) required by the ordinary contract, if \(d_i\)
has signal unit \(U_g\) and \(\omega_i\) has units \(U_g^{-2}\),
then \(n_p\) has \(U_g^{-1}\), \(q_p\) has \(U_g^{-2}\), and
\(\widehat m_p\) has \(U_g\). Under the additional conditions of
Equation~\eqref{eq:formal-diagonal-weight}, formal diagonal weight has
\(U_g^{-2}\) and covariance has \(U_g^2\). If \(\omega_i\) is instead a
dimensionless or otherwise scaled gridding weight, \(q_p\) inherits that
gridding unit and cannot be labeled inverse signal variance, while the
normalized signal still has \(U_g\).

The current project-level validated product convention is signal and kernel in
\(\mathrm{mJy/beam}\), formal weight in
\(1/(\mathrm{mJy/beam})^2\), noise variance in
\((\mathrm{mJy/beam})^2\), exposure coverage in seconds, and support,
validity, hits, and standardized products dimensionless. Other accepted
map-input unit tokens require an explicit cold-boundary conversion and matching
metadata. A \(\mathrm{Jy/pixel}\) conversion depends on pixel solid angle; a
\(\mathrm{mJy/beam}\) conversion requires beam identity. A standardized signal
formed with formal weight is not a calibrated statistical-significance map.
Empirical significance belongs to SCI-NOI-002.

\section{Observation coaddition}

\subsection{Full covariance estimator}

Stack normalized observation maps into \(\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{\rm stack}\)
with complete stacked response \(T_{\rm stack}\) and additive map bias
\(\boldsymbol b_{m,\rm stack}\). Define the observation-map error and its full
block covariance, including cross-observation blocks, by
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol e_{\rm stack}=\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{\rm stack}
 -T_{\rm stack}\boldsymbol s-\boldsymbol b_{m,\rm stack},\qquad
 C_{\rm stack}=\Cov(\boldsymbol e_{\rm stack}\mid
 \boldsymbol s,\theta).
 \label{eq:coadd-error-covariance}
\end{equation}
This is covariance of observation-map errors at fixed common sky/estimand, not
an ensemble covariance that counts shared stochastic sky structure as noise.
Before covariance inversion, let \(J\) select only compatible finite-valid
observation-pixel entries. Define
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol z=J\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{\rm stack},\qquad
 T=JT_{\rm stack},\qquad
 C_{\mathrm{obs}}=JC_{\rm stack}J^\trans,
 \qquad \E[\boldsymbol z]=T\boldsymbol s+\boldsymbol b_z,
 \quad \boldsymbol b_z=J\boldsymbol b_{m,\rm stack}.
 \label{eq:selected-coadd-system}
\end{equation}
Thus \(T\) includes each observation mapmaker response and any
registration/resampling. It is a stacked identity only when each observation
map already estimates the same explicitly named response-convolved (or other)
common map quantity with unit gain, and its grid, WCS, units, pixel basis,
validity domain, and centering convention match. The GLS coadd is
\begin{align}
 \widehat{\boldsymbol s}_{\mathrm{GLS}}
 &= (T^\trans C_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}T)^{+}
 T^\trans C_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol z-\boldsymbol b_z),
 \label{eq:coadd-gls}\\
 C_{\mathrm{coadd}}
 &= (T^\trans C_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}T)^{+}
 \label{eq:coadd-gls-covariance}
\end{align}
on the identifiable common subspace. For an arbitrary fixed coadd analysis
weight, covariance again uses full sandwich propagation.
The subtraction in Equation~\eqref{eq:coadd-gls} is authorized only when
\(\boldsymbol b_z\) is known and conditioned upon. If it is unknown, it either
remains as the explicit propagated bias \(B\boldsymbol b_z\) or enters the
model through identifiable nuisance columns; it is never silently set to zero.
Every displayed \(C_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}\) likewise requires a positive-
definite covariance on the selected stochastic subspace; deterministic map or
centering null modes are removed in an explicit basis before inversion.

\subsection{Pixelwise inverse-variance-like coadd}

For aligned observations, define the preselected contributing set
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal O_p=\{o:\;&s_{op}=1,\ v_{op}=1,\hbox{ and }u_{op},
 \widehat m_{op},\widehat k_{op}\\
 &\hbox{ satisfy their declared finite-valid rules}\},\\
 \chi_{op}&=\mathbf1[o\in\mathcal O_p].
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:coadd-contribution-set}
\end{equation}
where \(u_{op}\) is a declared finite nonnegative coadd weight. The sums below
iterate \(\mathcal O_p\) directly; multiplication of invalid values by zero is
not masking. The accumulated coadd numerator, normalization, and signal are
\begin{align}
 n^{c}_p &=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_p}u_{op}\widehat m_{op},
 \label{eq:coadd-numerator}\\
 q^{c}_p &=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_p}u_{op},
 \label{eq:coadd-denominator}\\
 \widehat s_p &=\frac{n^{c}_p}{q^{c}_p}
 \quad\hbox{for valid }q^{c}_p>0.
 \label{eq:coadd-map}
\end{align}
If the compatible observation maps have unit \(U_c\) and the coadd weights
have unit \(V\), then \([n^c_p]=V U_c\), \([q^c_p]=V\), and
\([\widehat s_p]=U_c\). In the inverse-variance specialization
\(V=U_c^{-2}\), so \([n^c_p]=U_c^{-1}\) and \([q^c_p]=U_c^{-2}\).
Pixelwise coaddition converts all maps to the common unit before these sums;
in the full estimator, \(T\) carries any declared unit conversion.
Let \(B\) be the corresponding fixed, conditioned linear operator from the
selected vector \(\boldsymbol z\) to the coadd. Its exact conditional
covariance and response are
\begin{align}
 C_{c}&=B C_{\mathrm{obs}}B^\trans,
 \label{eq:coadd-full-covariance}\\
 R_c&=B T,
 \label{eq:coadd-response}\\
 \boldsymbol b_c&=B\boldsymbol b_z,
 \qquad \E[B\boldsymbol z]=R_c\boldsymbol s+\boldsymbol b_c.
 \label{eq:coadd-affine-mean}
\end{align}
with the response mapping written for the chosen stack convention.

If observation errors at pixel \(p\) are independent, each map is unbiased for
the same scalar responded estimand with unit gain, and
\(u_{op}=\phi_{op}=1/\Var(\widehat m_{op})\), then
\begin{equation}
 \Var(\widehat s_p)=\frac{1}{\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_p}\phi_{op}},
 \qquad \phi^c_p=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_p}\phi_{op}.
 \label{eq:coadd-independent-precision}
\end{equation}
Thus the coadd weight equals summed observation inverse variance only under
those conditions. A gridding denominator substituted for \(\phi\) makes
Equation~\eqref{eq:coadd-map} a normalized weighted average, not an authorized
inverse-variance estimator.

For a positive-definite cross-observation covariance \(K_p\) on the
preselected observation set \(\mathcal O_p\), the minimum-variance unbiased
scalar combination has
\begin{align}
 \boldsymbol\alpha_p&=
 \frac{K_p^{-1}\boldsymbol1}{\boldsymbol1^\trans K_p^{-1}\boldsymbol1},
 &
 \phi^c_p&=\boldsymbol1^\trans K_p^{-1}\boldsymbol1.
 \label{eq:correlated-observation-coadd}
\end{align}
This precision is generally not the sum of marginal inverse variances, and
\(K_p^{-1}\boldsymbol1\) can yield negative coefficients even when \(K_p\) is
positive definite. Such a solution belongs to the full correlated-GLS branch,
not the preceding ordinary coadd restricted to nonnegative \(u_{op}\). A
singular \(K_p\) requires the same explicit stochastic-subspace treatment as
the full coadd. For two
observations with variances \(\sigma_1^2,\sigma_2^2\) and covariance \(c\),
\begin{align}
 \alpha_1&=\frac{\sigma_2^2-c}{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2-2c},&
 \alpha_2&=\frac{\sigma_1^2-c}{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2-2c},&
 \Var(\widehat s)&=
 \frac{\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^2-c^2}{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2-2c}.
 \label{eq:two-observation-coadd}
\end{align}
When cross-pixel covariance is consequential, the pixelwise formula is itself
an approximation to Equation~\eqref{eq:coadd-gls}.

The coadd kernel/response, exposure, and hits companions are
\begin{align}
 \widehat k^c_p&=
 \frac{\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_p}u_{op}\widehat k_{op}}{q^c_p},
 \label{eq:coadd-kernel}\\
 x^c_p&=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_p}x_{op},
 \label{eq:coadd-coverage}\\
 (h^\star)^c_p&=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O_p}h^\star_{op},
 \label{eq:coadd-hits}
\end{align}
where \(h^\star\) is the explicitly declared geometric or positive-weight hit
definition. These equations apply only when they are the declared product
definitions. A count of contributing
observations is another diagnostic and must not be confused with sample hits.
Coadd validity requires compatible units, frame/WCS, pixel geometry, map-group
identity, response convention, finite inputs, and positive normalization.

\subsection{Centering or mean subtraction}

No coadd may silently change the estimand by centering an observation. If a
fixed centering operator
\(L_o=I-\boldsymbol1\boldsymbol a_o^\trans\), with
\(\boldsymbol a_o^\trans\boldsymbol1=1\), is applied, the actual input is
\(L_o\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_o\), its covariance is
\(L_oC_{m,o}L_o^\trans\), and its response is \(L_oR_o\). The constant mode
then lies in the input/response null space because \(L_o\boldsymbol1=0\), and
the coadd estimates a centered sky, not the absolute map offset. More generally, with
\(L=\operatorname{blockdiag}(L_1,\ldots,L_O)\),
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\boldsymbol m}'_{\rm stack}=L\widehat{\boldsymbol m}_{\rm stack},
 \qquad T'_{\rm stack}=LT_{\rm stack},
 \qquad \boldsymbol b'_{m,\rm stack}=L\boldsymbol b_{m,\rm stack},
 \qquad C'_{\rm stack}=LC_{\rm stack}L^\trans.
 \label{eq:stacked-centering}
\end{equation}
Thus every cross-observation block becomes
\(C'_{or}=L_oC_{or}L_r^\trans\). Centered outputs obey
\(\boldsymbol a_o^\trans\widehat{\boldsymbol m}'_o=0\); for a positive-
definite uncentered covariance, \(\boldsymbol a_o\) is the corresponding
covariance null vector because \(L_o^\trans\boldsymbol a_o=0\). Before a
covariance inverse is attempted, the system is represented in an explicit
basis for \(\operatorname{range}(L)\). The response-null constant and
covariance-null \(\boldsymbol a_o\) coincide only for uniform centering.
Whether centering is requested, effective,
observation-resolved, or unconditional legacy behavior must be recorded. The
same operator must apply to signal, kernel/response, covariance, noise
realizations, and validation predictions.
