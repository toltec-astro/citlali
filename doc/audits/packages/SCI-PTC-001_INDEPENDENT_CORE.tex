\section{Typed scientific domain}
\label{sec:domain}

\subsection{Identities, shapes, and ordering}

Let an observation have immutable identifier $o$.  It contains ordered scans
$s\in\mathcal S_o$; each scan may contain processing segments or chunks
$g\in\mathcal G_{os}$.  Segment $g$ has an actual, possibly ragged extent
$T_{osg}$, not a nominal fixed size.  Detector identity $d\in\mathcal D_o$ is
an immutable APT-backed UID, never merely a transient column number.  Stage
$q$ is an immutable product identity, not an adjective inferred from a path.
The full datum identity is
\begin{equation}
  i=(o,s,g,t,d,q),\qquad 0\leq t<T_{osg}.
  \label{eq:datum-id}
\end{equation}
Every array product \req{} serialize its axes, lengths, storage order, and the
maps from integer positions to these identities.  A consumer shall not infer
detector order from an APT row order, sample order from filename order, or
stage from a directory name.  Any flattening uses a declared bijection
$\nu(i)$; mathematical equations are independent of that storage choice.

The sample timestamp is $\tau_{osgt}$ in a named time scale with declared
epoch and units.  Its nominal rate is $f_{osg}$, while actual timestamps and
gaps remain authoritative.  Resampling/filter semantics and input-output time
registration belong to the upstream response record.  PTC must preserve or
explicitly replace that record when it changes timing.  Scan and segment
boundaries are causal boundaries unless the realized state explicitly records
cross-boundary support.

For a coherent segment, the admitted detector timestream is a matrix
\begin{equation}
 X^{(q)}_{osg}=\bigl[x_{td}\bigr]
 \in (U_q\cup\{\mathrm{missing}\})^{T_{osg}\times D_{osg}},
 \label{eq:input-shape}
\end{equation}
where $U_q$ is the declared signal unit at stage $q$.  Per-detector units are
permitted only with an explicit calibration map to a common fitting unit.
PTC coefficients and weights must state whether they act before or after that
map.  A detector timestream is in the detector/readout signal frame; a source
mask additionally identifies the sky-coordinate frame, projection, epoch,
pointing product, and registration used to generate it.  Correlated-mode
coefficients themselves have no sky-coordinate frame, but inherit the time,
detector, unit, and stage identities of their operands.

\subsection{Validity, flags, missing values, and influence}

For each input datum define:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item direct validity $v_i\in\{0,1\}$;
  \item a set-valued cause flag $\phi_i$ whose vocabulary and severity are
  versioned;
  \item a set-valued upstream influence record $\iota_i$, possibly represented
  compactly as intervals, kernels, or segment support;
  \item finite predicate $n_i=\mathbf 1\{x_i\in\R\}$; and
  \item scientific eligibility $e_i$, which is not identical to any one of
  the preceding fields.
\end{itemize}
Missing and non-finite are distinct: missing means no value is present;
non-finite is a present IEEE value outside $\R$.  Neither may silently enter a
fit, normalization, covariance estimate, convergence statistic, or detector
weight.  Replacement or synthesis may create a finite computational value,
but cannot restore scientific eligibility.  Cause flags are monotone under
processing unless a new stage preserves the old causes and records a justified
resolution state.  Unknown flag values and unknown validity semantics fail
closed.

For any output component $z_j$, causal influence is the transitive dependency
set
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I_j = \bigcup_{i:\,i\xrightarrow{\mathrm{dep}} j}
   \left(\{i\}\cup\iota_i\right),
 \label{eq:influence-set}
\end{equation}
where $i\xrightarrow{\mathrm{dep}}j$ means the realized algorithm allowed $x_i$ to affect
$z_j$, a fitted parameter applied to $z_j$, a selection decision governing
$z_j$, or a weight governing its later use.  This definition includes
indirect fit-and-apply influence even when $\partial z_j/\partial x_i=0$ at a
particular numeric point.  Compact over-approximations are acceptable;
under-approximations are not.  Direct validity describes $j$ itself, while
$\mathcal I_j$ describes what could have contaminated it.

\subsection{Input assumptions and admission}

The minimum input bundle is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal X=(X,V,\Phi,\mathcal I,A,M,K_x,Q,\Pi),
 \label{eq:input-bundle}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is immutable APT state, $M$ is the collection of masks, $K_x$ is the
complete conditioned upstream response or an explicit unavailable state,
$Q$ is stage identity, and $\Pi$ contains parent/processing provenance and
timing semantics.  Admission to a fit is an explicit Boolean function
\begin{equation}
 a_i=A_{\rm fit}(v_i,n_i,\phi_i,\iota_i,M_i,A_d,q,
                  \text{resolved state})\in\{0,1\}.
 \label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
Admission to subtraction, output retention, empirical estimation, simulation,
and downstream science are separate functions.  One mask bit cannot safely
stand for all five.  Every mask records polarity (included versus excluded),
domain, generating stage, coordinate/time registration, boundary convention,
and whether it controls fit, application, output, or feedback.

\subsection{Four state layers}

\midrule

\section{Abstract correlated-mode operator}
\label{sec:operator}

\subsection{Data-dependent and stateful definition}

Let $m$ collect masks/admission fields, $a$ collect APT and calibration state,
and $r$ collect effective/resolved controls.  For pass or iteration $k$,
\begin{align}
 \theta_k &= T_k(x,m,a,r,h_k),
   \label{eq:theta-fit}\\
 z_k &= F_k(x,m,a,r,\theta_k,h_k),
   \label{eq:clean-op}\\
 h_{k+1} &= G_k(h_k,x,m,a,r,\theta_k,z_k),
   \label{eq:state-update}\\
 w_k &= W_k(z_k,m,a,r,\theta_k,h_{k+1}).
   \label{eq:weight-op}
\end{align}
Here $x=\operatorname{vec}(X)$, $\theta_k$ contains all fitted or selected
cleaning quantities, $h_k$ is immutable input state for the iteration/pass,
$z_k$ is cleaned TOD, and $w_k$ is a named weighting object.  The final
product is
\begin{equation}
 (z,w,\Lambda)=\mathcal P(x,m,a,R;E,O,h_0),
 \label{eq:ptc-product}
\end{equation}
with realized state $\Lambda$ sufficient to replay the fixed branch or declare why
replay is impossible.  Equations~\eqref{eq:theta-fit}--\eqref{eq:ptc-product}
make clear that conditioning on a realized $\theta$ is different from
propagating the randomness and data dependence of $\theta(x)$.

Feedback includes any use of $z_k$, its map, a source estimate, flags,
detector statistics, or convergence statistic to construct $m$, $a$,
$\theta_{k+1}$, or $h_{k+1}$.  Each feedback edge has a parent product, pass
number, causal support, and stop rule.  A second pass is a new realized state,
not an in-place relabeling of the first pass.

\subsection{Conditioned linear-mode special case}

Many mean, common-mode, template-regression, or PCA cleaners reduce, after
selection is frozen, to a fitted-mode subtraction.  Let $y\in\R^N$ contain
the admitted values, $B\in\R^{N\times r}$ the realized mode/template design,
and $\Omega$ the declared positive-semidefinite fit coefficient matrix.  With an explicitly
defined generalized inverse,
\begin{align}
 \widehat c &= (B^T\Omega B)^{+}B^T\Omega y,
   \label{eq:mode-coeff}\\
 \widehat u &= B\widehat c,
   \label{eq:mode-estimate}\\
 z &= L_{B,\Omega}y,
 \quad L_{B,\Omega}=I-B(B^T\Omega B)^{+}B^T\Omega.
   \label{eq:linear-cleaner}
\end{align}
The contract records the construction and units of $y$, $B$, and $\Omega$;
centering/scaling; rank $r$; singular-value or eigenvalue tolerance;
pseudoinverse convention; retained and excluded modes; ordering and tie
rules; coefficient normalization; fit support; and subtraction support.
Basis sign, rotation within a degenerate subspace, and reciprocal rescaling of
$B$ and $c$ are gauge freedoms.  A physical claim attaches to the projected
subspace and $B\widehat c$, not to an unpinned coefficient sign.

If $B$, $\Omega$, rank, support, or selection is estimated from $y$, then
Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-cleaner} is only a conditioned operator.  It is not
a global linear transfer for the full procedure.  Robust means, medians,
clipping, component thresholds, detector rejection, convergence branches,
and source-mask feedback have the same consequence.

\subsection{Mean/common-mode and PCA identities}

A weighted common mode at time $t$ has the generic form
\begin{equation}
 \widehat q_t=
 \frac{\sum_d a_{td}\alpha_{td}\,\widetilde x_{td}}
      {\sum_d a_{td}\alpha_{td}},
 \label{eq:common-mode}
\end{equation}
only when the denominator is finite and nonzero.  The interface must identify
$\widetilde x$ (raw, centered, calibrated, normalized, or residual), the
coefficient $\alpha$, its unit and provenance, and whether the same support is
used when fitting and subtracting.  A median or robust location is a distinct
nonlinear estimator and cannot be documented by Equation~\eqref{eq:common-mode}.

For PCA/SVD, the realized matrix, missing-data policy, centering, scaling,
orientation, covariance/Gram definition, component ordering, rank threshold,
and tie convention determine the subspace.  ``Remove $r$ components'' is not
sufficient.  The zero-rank and full-rank cases, and $N<D$ versus $N\ge D$,
are explicitly defined.  A component excluded from subtraction may still
have influenced selection and must appear in realized state.

\subsection{Iteration and convergence}

The realized trace is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H=(h_0,\theta_0,z_0,C_0,\ldots,h_K,\theta_K,z_K,C_K),
 \label{eq:iteration-trace}
\end{equation}
where $C_k$ is the exact convergence statistic and decision.  The contract
states maximum iterations, absolute/relative tolerance and unit, norm/support,
NaN/empty behavior, minimum iteration count, tie comparison, converged versus
exhausted state, and which $z_k$ is emitted.  Oscillation or rank changes are
not silently called convergence.  One-pass and two-pass results have distinct
parents and realized identities.

Sequential and OpenMP execution are equivalent only if they consume the same
resolved inputs and random streams and produce the same admitted identities,
selected subspace, ranks, convergence decisions, validity/influence causes,
stage/provenance, and scientific conclusions.  A predeclared numeric tolerance
may cover floating reduction order for continuous values; it may not excuse a
branch, component-count, mask, flag, or eligibility difference.  Deterministic
tie and sign conventions are required where roundoff could alter selection.

\section{Expectation, uncertainty, and covariance}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

\subsection{Estimator expectation and bias}

Let the admitted input be
\begin{equation}
 x=\mu(\beta,\gamma)+\epsilon,
 \qquad \E[\epsilon\mid\mathcal C]=0,
 \qquad \Cov(\epsilon\mid\mathcal C)=C_x,
 \label{eq:data-model}
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ is the scientific signal, $\gamma$ denotes calibration and
systematic nuisance quantities, and $\mathcal C$ is the declared conditioning
set.  Let $\eta(x)$ collect every fitted, selected, or feedback quantity
entering the final operation, including $\theta(x)$, any data-derived mask,
state branch, and weight that feeds back.  For the complete data-dependent
cleaner $f(x)=F_{\rm tot}(x,\eta(x))$,
\begin{equation}
 \E[z\mid\beta,\gamma,\mathcal C]
 =\int f\!\left(\mu(\beta,\gamma)+e\right)
        p(e\mid\mathcal C)\,de,
 \label{eq:exact-expectation}
\end{equation}
which generally differs from $f(\mu)$.  The estimator bias relative to a
declared target response $R_\star\beta$ is
\begin{equation}
 b_z(\beta)=\E[z\mid\beta,\mathcal C]-R_\star\beta.
 \label{eq:estimator-bias}
\end{equation}
No zero-bias claim follows merely because the fitted correlated component has
zero sample mean.

For a fixed realized branch at a differentiable point $x_0$, the complete
Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
 J_x=\left.\frac{\partial F_{\rm tot}}{\partial x}\right|_{\eta}
     +\left.\frac{\partial F_{\rm tot}}{\partial\eta}\right|_{x}
       \frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x},
 \label{eq:complete-jacobian}
\end{equation}
including mode, weight, mask, and feedback dependence where differentiable.
At threshold, rank, clipping, mask, or convergence discontinuities a unique
Jacobian may not exist.  The response is then represented by declared
one-sided/finite perturbations on a stated domain or is \unknown.

\subsection{Formal covariance}

If all fitted/selected state is conditioned upon and the cleaner is the fixed
linear operator $L$, the conditional formal covariance is
\begin{equation}
 C_{z\mid L}=L C_x L^T.
 \label{eq:conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
This matrix generally has temporal and inter-detector off-diagonal terms even
when $C_x$ is diagonal.  For the complete differentiable procedure, the
first-order covariance is
\begin{equation}
 C_z^{(1)}=J_x C_x J_x^T
   +J_\gamma C_\gamma J_\gamma^T
   +J_x C_{x\gamma}J_\gamma^T
   +J_\gamma C_{\gamma x}J_x^T,
 \label{eq:delta-covariance}
\end{equation}
with calibration/systematic covariance and cross-covariance included when
applicable.  If $\eta$ is treated as an explicit random variable, the exact
law of total covariance is
\begin{equation}
 \Cov(z\mid\mathcal C)=
 \E\!\left[\Cov(z\mid\eta,\mathcal C)\mid\mathcal C\right]
 +\Cov\!\left(\E[z\mid\eta,\mathcal C]\mid\mathcal C\right).
 \label{eq:total-covariance}
\end{equation}
Using only the first term understates uncertainty whenever fitted modes,
selection, or weights vary.  A covariance product states whether it is exact,
conditional, first-order, empirical, modeled, or an approximation; its axes,
units $U_z^2$, normalization, support, rank, and omitted correlations are
machine-readable.

Observation processing does not prove cross-observation independence.  For
observations $o,o'$, $C_z^{oo'}$ may be nonzero through common calibration,
atmosphere, APT errors, shared fitted models, or processing choices.  An absent
cross-observation block means unknown, not zero.

\subsection{Formal variance, empirical scatter, and systematic uncertainty}

Formal marginal variance is the diagonal of a declared covariance model,
\begin{equation}
 \sigma^2_{i,\mathrm{formal}}=(C_z)_{ii}.
 \label{eq:formal-variance}
\end{equation}
Empirical scatter is a statistic of a declared residual/support, for example
\begin{equation}
 \widehat s_d^2=
 \frac{1}{\nu_d}\sum_{t\in\mathcal A_d}
   \rho_{td}\bigl(z_{td}-\widehat\mu_d\bigr)^2,
 \label{eq:empirical-scatter}
\end{equation}
with estimator, centering, coefficients $\rho$, effective degrees of freedom
$\nu_d$, correlation correction, finite/flag policy, and lifecycle recorded.
It is not automatically an unbiased variance estimate, and $1/\widehat s_d^2$
is not automatically a precision.  Calibration/systematic uncertainty is a
nuisance covariance or bounded error with identity and correlation domain; it
must not be folded into empirical scatter without labeling.  Response
uncertainty concerns uncertainty in how sky signal is transformed and remains
distinct from residual-noise covariance.

\subsection{Detector weights and normalization}

Let $q_d=H_d(z,m,a,\theta,\Lambda)$ be a declared detector statistic and
\begin{align}
 \widetilde w_d &= g(q_d),
   \label{eq:raw-detector-weight}\\
 w_d &= \kappa(\widetilde w,\mathcal D_{\rm sel})\,\widetilde w_d.
   \label{eq:normalized-detector-weight}
\end{align}
The interface records $H_d$, $g$, fit support, selected detector set,
zero/negative/non-finite behavior, clipping, unit, normalization $\kappa$,
normalization domain, and whether weights are fixed, fitted, selected, or
data-derived.  Recomputing a weight creates a new realized product and cannot
silently alter an earlier stage.  If $\widetilde w_d=\sigma_d^{-2}$ it has
units $U_z^{-2}$; common normalizations may make $w_d$ dimensionless and
destroy an absolute-precision interpretation while preserving relative
gridding coefficients.

A weight may be called marginal precision only if the target random variable,
conditioning set, covariance, units, estimator, and lifecycle are stated and
\begin{equation}
 w_d=\left[\Var(\widehat y_d\mid\mathcal C)\right]^{-1}
 \label{eq:marginal-precision}
\end{equation}
with no omitted normalization.  Combining such scalars as independent
precision additionally requires the applicable covariance matrix to be
diagonal on the selected support.  A detector weight cannot represent
temporal covariance, inter-detector covariance, pixel coupling, or
cross-observation covariance unless an explicit operator says how.

\section{Response and transfer}
\label{sec:response}

\subsection{Complete conditioned response}

Let $s$ be a small physical sky-signal perturbation in a named amplitude,
spatial, temporal, and stage domain.  The admitted upstream data have complete
conditioned response $K_x$ or explicit unavailable state:
\begin{equation}
 \delta x=K_x\,\delta s.
 \label{eq:upstream-response}
\end{equation}
At a differentiable realized PTC state, the complete local response is
\begin{equation}
 K_{z,\mathrm{loc}}(x_0)=J_x(x_0)K_x.
 \label{eq:local-response}
\end{equation}
It includes fit, selection, mask, weight-feedback, iteration, resampling, and
all upstream response-changing stages.  A partial kernel must not be labeled
as this complete response.  The response record identifies the conditioning
state, perturbation convention and amplitude, causal support, time/rate,
phase convention, detector identities, units, boundary handling, validity
domain, numerical/analytic method, and uncertainty.  If $K_x$ is unavailable,
or any response-changing PTC term is omitted, $K_z$ is unavailable.

Response covariance or uncertainty is represented separately, e.g.
\begin{equation}
 C_{K_z}=\Cov\!\left(\operatorname{vec}K_z\mid\mathcal C\right),
 \label{eq:response-covariance}
\end{equation}
or a declared low-rank/bounded approximation with omitted directions.  A
deterministic kernel file without uncertainty is not proof of zero response
uncertainty.

\subsection{When a global transfer exists}

A single global linear transfer $K_z$ independent of data exists only on a
declared domain where all of the following hold: admission, masks, selected
detectors/components/rank, coefficients or mode basis, iteration count and
branch, weights that feed back, boundary rules, and upstream response are
fixed; the mapping is linear; and time/space variation is represented in the
operator.  Shift invariance is additionally required before a scalar Fourier
transfer function is meaningful.  If these conditions fail, only a realized
local response, a family of conditioned responses, or \unknown{} is truthful.

The principal response dimensions are:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.22\linewidth,style=nextline]
  \item[Temporal and phase.] Impulse support, timestamp registration, gaps,
  rate changes, segment edges, and any noncausal support are explicit.  A
  sinusoidal gain/phase curve is valid only for a fixed linear time-invariant
  region.
  \item[Amplitude.] Clipping, robust weights, rank/component thresholds,
  source selection, and convergence create amplitude-dependent or
  discontinuous response.  Perturbation amplitude and side are recorded.
  \item[Spatial/source mask.] The fit mask and application mask may differ.
  Response depends on source position, pointing registration, mask dilation,
  and boundary convention; crossing a derived-mask boundary can be
  discontinuous.
  \item[Common/PCA and detector.] Correlated-mode subtraction is a mixing
  operator over samples and detectors.  It can attenuate any sky component
  overlapping the selected subspace and induce detector covariance.
  \item[Extended emission.] A mode common across detectors can be astronomical
  rather than atmospheric.  No point-source response establishes response to
  extended modes.
  \item[Iteration/pass.] The response is the derivative or finite perturbation
  of the complete recurrence, including feedback and stop decisions, not a
  product of only the visible fixed filters.
  \item[Map/beam.] Projection and gridding compose with Equation
  \eqref{eq:local-response}; beam amplitude/shape and map significance require
  the corresponding response and covariance, not only cleaned TOD.
\end{description}

\section{Product and provenance contract}
\label{sec:products}

Every raw, outer, inner, full, mini, diagnostic, simulated, and processed
product has an immutable product UID, exact stage enum, observation/scan/
segment/sample/detector extents, axes and ordering, signal units and frame,
time/rate identity, flags/validity/influence policy, requested/effective/
resolved/realized state digests, and atomic creation status.  It links to:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item immutable direct parent product UID(s) and content identity;
  \item processing activity UID, application commit, configuration/state
  digests, and algorithm/schema versions;
  \item APT/calibration/mask/pointing/response inputs by immutable identity;
  \item prior pass or simulation truth parent where applicable; and
  \item cleaned TOD, weight, response, covariance, diagnostic, and provenance
  siblings by typed relation, never by a guessed filename.
\end{enumerate}

A product is published atomically only after required siblings and links are
durable.  Partial output is explicitly incomplete and scientifically
ineligible.  Full/mini products carry actual per-chunk start, stop, count, and
global-index mapping; a final short chunk is not padded into scientific
coverage.  Diagnostics never inherit science-product identity.  Simulated
products identify truth, injected response, random generator/version, master
seed, stream/substream mapping, and whether fitted state was learned from
truth-contaminated data.

The realized record contains, at minimum: method branch; candidate and
selected detectors/samples/components; every threshold and its unit; fit and
application masks; centering/scaling; fitted basis/coefficient identities;
rank/degeneracy/tie decisions; excluded modes; all passes and parents;
iteration trace and convergence/exhaustion; empirical detector statistics and
weights before/after normalization; replacements/synthesis and propagated
causes; response availability and identity; covariance availability and
approximation class; and RNG state.  A digest without a resolvable canonical
record is insufficient provenance.

\section{Provisional fail-closed consumer contracts}
\label{sec:consumers}

These are boundary conditions, not audits or launches of the consumers.

\subsection{VAL eligibility}

VAL may admit a PTC product only when immutable identity and parent links,
actual extents, units/time/detector mapping, direct validity, cause-preserving
influence, realized state, complete response or an explicitly response-free
validation purpose, and required covariance/approximation are available.
Replacement/synthesis or influenced samples are excluded from scientific
validation.  An unknown state is not a pass.  Validation of file readability
or numerical finiteness does not establish scientific eligibility.

\subsection{MAP weights and significance}

For projection/design matrix $P$, diagonal coefficient matrix $W$, and
cleaned vector $z$, ordinary normalized gridding has
\begin{align}
 \widehat m &= A z,
 \quad A=(P^TWP)^{+}P^TW,
   \label{eq:map-estimator}\\
 C_m &= A C_z A^T.
   \label{eq:map-covariance}
\end{align}
If the projection support, selection, or gridding coefficients are themselves
data-derived, let $\xi(x)$ collect them.  The complete local map response is
then
\begin{equation}
 J_m=A\,J_x+
 \left.\frac{\partial\{A(\xi)z\}}{\partial\xi}\right|_{z}
 \frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x},
 \qquad K_{m,\mathrm{loc}}=J_m K_x.
 \label{eq:map-local-response}
\end{equation}
The second term vanishes only when those quantities are fixed on the response
domain; omitting it does not produce a complete response.
The entries or diagonal of $P^TWP$ are normalization/gridding weights by
default.  In the one-hot, independent selected-sample special case with
$W=\diag(\sigma_i^{-2})$ in absolute units, the corresponding denominator is
marginal precision.  Otherwise no inverse-variance or significance claim
follows.  A truthful correlated GLS estimator would require
\begin{equation}
 \widehat m_{\rm GLS}=(P^TC_z^{-1}P)^{+}P^TC_z^{-1}z,
 \qquad
 C_{m,\rm GLS}=(P^TC_z^{-1}P)^{+},
 \label{eq:gls-reference}
\end{equation}
under its rank/model conditions, but this equation is a reference contract,
not authorization or evidence of a GLS implementation.  Cross-observation
coadds likewise treat their coefficients as nonprecision until registered
observation covariance and independence/precision conditions are proven.

\subsection{NOI noise and covariance}

NOI receives the exact PTC stage, selection, masks, response, weights, and
realized cleaning state.  Residual TOD is not automatically a stationary or
independent noise realization: fitted correlated modes, source leakage,
selection, flags, and feedback can bias its spectrum and covariance.  NOI
must identify which temporal, detector, pixel, and cross-observation
correlations are represented, approximated, or unavailable.  A diagonal PSD
or detector scatter cannot be promoted to the full $C_z$ without a falsifiable
approximation and validity domain.

\subsection{BEAM response and uncertainty}

BEAM remains unlaunched.  Any later beam consumer requires the complete
realized local PTC response over source position and amplitude, pointing and
mask registration, causal support/phase/rate, calibration coupling, and
response uncertainty.  A point-source transfer does not establish extended
response; a fixed global kernel is unavailable when data-derived cleaning or
mask feedback changes the operator.  BEAM-relevant facts are to be routed
through the later VAL handoff and owner brief, not a new BEAM artifact here.

unavailable.  This does not by itself prohibit bounded existing production;
