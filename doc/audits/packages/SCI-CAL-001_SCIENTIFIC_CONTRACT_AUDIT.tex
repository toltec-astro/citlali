\begin{abstract}
The frozen independent contract requires a detector-identity join, a declared
calibration pivot, a line-of-sight extinction operator, explicit target-unit
and beam/template meaning, and separate statistical and systematic
calibration covariance.  At exact source SHA \GoverningSHA, the multiplicative
detector flux-scale path is structurally simple, the Rayleigh--Jeans convention
is explicitly recorded, and calibration precedes the response kernel and
subsequent RTC operations.  The assessed implementation is nevertheless
\emph{nonconformant}.  Its atmospheric factor is the exponential of a
zenith-equivalent opacity rather than the required opacity times airmass
relative to a fixed pivot; its lowest-opacity model returns no extinction for
finite positive 225-GHz opacity; non-finite inputs and factors are not
consistently rejected; and external APT coefficients are attached to raw TOD
columns by position without proving detector identity.  Calibration,
extinction, beam, and common-mode covariance are not propagated, while
non-default unit/beam meanings and realized provenance remain unapproved or
incomplete.  The package identity is coherent, so the verdict is
\code{amend}.  The proposed status is \code{restricted_use}: only explicitly
extinction-disabled, \code{mJy/beam}, relative or diagnostic use may proceed
under an independently verified detector/APT identity; extinction-enabled,
non-default-unit, absolute-photometry, covariance, significance, and feedback
claims fail closed pending owner decisions, repair, same-SHA evidence, and
fresh re-audit.  This report proposes a disposition; it is not production
authorization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen contract and audit equations}

The complete independent derivation is incorporated by reference to the
frozen path and digest above.  Its identities use observation \(o\), stable
detector UID \(i\), ordered aligned sample \(t\), and array \(a(i)\).  A
calibration record is joined by UID, never by row position.  For detector gain
\(g_{oi}\), target-unit transfer \(q_{oati}\), zenith opacity
\(\tau_{oat}\), airmass \(X(e_{ot})\), and a fixed coefficient pivot
\(X^{\rm ref}_{oi}\), the required multiplier is
\begin{equation}
 C_{oti}=q_{oati}g_{oi}
 \exp\!\left[\tau_{oat}\{X(e_{ot})-X^{\rm ref}_{oi}\}\right].
 \label{eq:audit-required-factor}
\end{equation}
The calibrated signal and a synthetic response companion obey
\begin{align}
 y_{oti}&=m_{oti}C_{oti}(x_{oti}-b_{oti}),
 \label{eq:audit-required-signal}\\
 K_{oti,r}&=m_{oti}C_{oti}k_{oti,r}.
 \label{eq:audit-required-response}
\end{align}
These are the final-audit restatements of the independently numbered
multiplier, signal-estimator, and kernel-transfer equations in the frozen
core.  A measured detector channel is not a response kernel.  If the kernel is
constructed after calibration as a unit source already expressed in the final
coefficient basis, that alternate ordering is admissible only when its
Jacobian identity and unit are stated and preserved through every later
operator.

Conditional sample covariance transforms as
\begin{equation}
 \Cov(y\mid C,m)=M C\,\Cov(x\mid C,m)\,C^{\mathsf T}M^{\mathsf T},
 \label{eq:audit-conditional-covariance}
\end{equation}
while the full covariance adds the low-rank and correlated terms induced by
detector relative gain, array/common absolute scale, extinction, beam,
bandpass, and template uncertainty.  Frozen-core labels
\code{eq:log-factor-perturbation} through \code{eq:full-covariance} define
that decomposition.  Merely relabeling downstream inverse variance
after multiplying samples does not add calibration-systematic covariance.

The required state lifecycle is one way:
\begin{equation}
 \hbox{requested}\longrightarrow\hbox{effective}
 \longrightarrow\hbox{observation-resolved}\longrightarrow\hbox{realized}.
 \label{eq:audit-state-lifecycle}
\end{equation}
Realized state includes coefficient-table identity and digest, UID join,
factor decomposition and pivot, tau source and validity interval, airmass
model, per-sample correction range, target unit, beam/pixel/frequency basis,
rejected support, covariance components, and response identity.

\section{Implementation conformity trace}

\subsection{Realized operator}

For elevation \(e_t\), the implementation first computes
\begin{align}
 A_t &= \sec z_t\left[1-0.0012\{\sec^2 z_t-1\}\right],
 \qquad z_t=\frac{\pi}{2}-e_t,\\
 T_{at} &= p_{am}(e_t)\exp(-A_t\tau_{225}),\\
 \widehat\tau_{at} &= -\frac{\log T_{at}}{A_t}.
 \label{eq:audit-code-tau}
\end{align}
The code then applies \(\exp(\widehat\tau_{at})\), yielding
\begin{equation}
 y^{\rm impl}_{ti}=x_{ti}\,f_i\,q_{a(i)}
 T_{a(i)t}^{-1/A_t},
 \label{eq:audit-code-signal}
\end{equation}
where \(f_i\) is APT \code{flxscale} and \(q_a\) is the requested-unit
conversion.  For a top-of-atmosphere coefficient the required atmospheric
factor is \(T^{-1}=\exp(A_t\widehat\tau)\); for a fixed nonzero pivot it is
\(\exp[\widehat\tau(A_t-X^{\rm ref})]\).  The implemented exponent of one
therefore implies the impossible sample-dependent pivot
\(X^{\rm ref}=A_t-1\).  It cannot implement Equation
\eqref{eq:audit-required-factor} for a fixed calibration reference over a scan.

For the lowest-opacity selection, \code{am_q0}, all three band coefficient
vectors are identically zero and \code{calc_tau} returns zero without using
the supplied elevation or positive \(\tau_{225}\).  Model thresholds derived
from the hard-coded 80-degree reference transmissions are
\(0,0.0504874,0.0883394,0.1583132,0.3048684\).  Thus every finite
\(0<\tau_{225}<0.0504874\) selects \code{am_q0} and receives exactly unity
extinction correction.

\section{Units, response, covariance, lifecycle, and provenance}

\subsection{Target-unit and beam transfer}

The accepted tokens are \code{mJy/beam}, \code{MJy/sr}, \code{uK}, and
\code{Jy/pixel}.  The active numbered configurations request
\code{mJy/beam}.  The non-default paths use one array-average circularized
FWHM,
\begin{equation}
 \bar\theta_a=\frac{\overline{\theta_{a,\rm major}}+
 \overline{\theta_{a,\rm minor}}}{2},\qquad
 \Omega_a^{\rm impl}=2\pi(\bar\theta_a/2.355)^2,
 \label{eq:audit-impl-beam}
\end{equation}
rather than a declared per-detector or effective product beam.  For an
elliptical Gaussian with axes \(a,b\), this circularization changes the area
relative to \(2\pi(a/2.355)(b/2.355)\) by
\((a+b)^2/(4ab)\).  Whether that is an approved product-beam approximation,
and whether point or integrated photometry may consume it, is an owner
decision.  The implementation does explicitly label \code{uK} as
monochromatic Rayleigh--Jeans brightness temperature at the nominal array
center frequency.  It supplies no bandpass/color correction, thermodynamic
temperature claim, or associated uncertainty; none may be inferred.

\subsection{Response companion and downstream weighting}

Signal is calibrated and extinction-corrected before a Gaussian, Airy, or
FITS kernel is created.  The kernel is therefore a final-unit, unit-source
response convention rather than a raw-domain companion transformed by
Equation~\eqref{eq:audit-required-response}.  Subsequent RTC filtering treats
signal and kernel alike.  This ordering can be coherent, but the products do
not persist the claimed input-template unit, coefficient pivot, or Jacobian;
the TOD kernel is marked \code{N/A} while map products give it the signal
unit.  Its numerical meaning is consequently an unapproved interface, not a
demonstrated physical detector response.

The scalar \code{fcf} carried to PTC contains the unit factor and the mean
implemented extinction factor, but excludes the detector's applied
\code{flxscale}.  Approximate/hybrid PTC weighting combines it with APT
\code{sens}.  That may be coherent only under a stated sensitivity
normalization and covariance convention.  Separately, RTC despike replacement
uses APT \code{responsivity} after flux calibration by dividing donor samples
and multiplying the target replacement; the APT declares no responsivity unit
or reciprocal convention.  Because replaced samples can influence later
filters, the ambiguity is material even though RTC's despike estimator remains
outside CAL.

\subsection{Uncertainty and state assessment}

No assessed CAL object carries uncertainties for \code{flxscale}, common
absolute scale, tau, model interpolation, beam axes, nominal frequency,
bandpass/color transfer, or their correlation.  Beammap fit products contain
shape-error columns, but CAL does not consume them.  The timestream statistical
weights may be rescaled by downstream logic; that is not the systematic term
required by frozen-core label \code{eq:full-covariance}.  Absolute
calibration and significance consumers therefore lack a complete uncertainty
contract.

Requested/effective booleans and unit tokens are represented, and the
observation-selected extinction model is mirrored into typed shadow state.
The realized product record remains incomplete.  TOD output can contain APT
columns and unit conversion constants and records an APT basename and mean
tau.  Map primary metadata records the APT basename, requested unit,
configuration booleans, conversion constants, and \code{MEAN_TAU}.  Neither
path proves the APT bytes/digest and UID join or records the applied
per-sample factor range, fixed pivot, exact airmass/model coefficients, q0
selection consequence, per-observation flux-scale correction, rejected
support, response identity, or calibration covariance.  Recomputing a mean
tau at mean elevation is not a lossless record of a time-varying multiplier;
defaulting failed metadata to a harmless-looking value cannot establish
valid execution.

\section{Findings by controlled class}

Every finding is open.  An observed finding names behavior present in the
exact governing source; its consequences may additionally be derived.  No
finding is closed by historical reductions or this audit.

\subsection{Implementation defects}

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F001 --- P0, observed and derived, high confidence.}
The extinction application omits the airmass-relative exponent.  The code
derives zenith-equivalent band opacity as \(-\log(T)/A\) and applies
\(\exp(\widehat\tau)\), which implies a sample-dependent coefficient pivot
and cannot equal Equation~\eqref{eq:audit-required-factor} for any fixed pivot
over a scan.  Under the conventional top-of-atmosphere target, deterministic
under-corrections range from sub-percent at high elevation to tens of percent
at low elevation for representative opacities.  This is an invalid-success
risk in the active extinction-enabled OOF, Science, and Beammap modes.

\emph{Closure gate:} after an owner approves opacity identity, calibration
pivot, airmass law, and validity domain, repair the factor and its metadata;
pass analytic elevation/tau grids, signal/kernel/weight propagation, paired
same-SHA Unity reductions, and an exact-successor re-audit.

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F002 --- P0, observed and derived, high confidence.}
For every finite \(0<\tau_{225}<0.0504874\), model selection chooses
\code{am_q0}; \code{calc_tau} then returns three zero vectors and the applied
extinction factor is exactly one.  Positive opacity and elevation are ignored.
No scientific authority or validation establishes that all TolTEC-band
attenuation vanishes in this interval.

\emph{Closure gate:} the calibration/atmosphere owner must approve a continuous
low-opacity model or an explicitly bounded zero-correction policy.  Test the
zero limit, both sides of every threshold, continuity/monotonicity, and
positive low-tau cases before successor re-audit.

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F003 --- P0, observed, high confidence.}
The CAL boundary does not fail closed on all invalid state.  Setup omits length
and finite/nonzero checks for \code{flxscale}, \code{sens}, and
\code{responsivity}; beam averages accept non-finite or nonpositive axes;
calibration checks coefficient length but not value; the extinction guard
rejects only \(\tau<0\) at one mean elevation, so NaN passes; per-sample
elevation, airmass, polynomial transmission, log domain, tau, and applied
factor are not required finite/in-domain.  Invalid values may propagate into
TOD, weights, and products rather than making the required operation fail.

\emph{Closure gate:} define validity for every selected coefficient and sample,
validate before mutation, reject invalid required state with a nonzero CLI
failure, preserve explicit invalidity in optional outputs, and pass NaN,
infinity, zero, negative, low-elevation, and nonpositive-transmission fixtures.

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F004 --- P0, observed, high confidence.}
External APT coefficients are attached to TOD columns by position.  Raw KIDs
interfaces are sorted numerically and their channel matrices are appended in
that order; the APT is filtered by network values while preserving its own row
order.  The code reads every raw \code{Header.Toltec.RoachIndex} into a vector
but never compares or uses it, and no detector UID is joined to raw columns.
Count equality and within-group contiguity cannot detect a permuted network or
detector table.  A same-sized, contiguous but differently ordered APT can
therefore silently apply the wrong detector calibration.

\emph{Closure gate:} approve and implement one stable detector identity join
with unique/missing/duplicate checks and explicit network/header constraints;
make row order a validated consequence rather than authority.  Pass
permutation, interface/header disagreement, duplicate UID, missing UID, and
round-trip provenance tests plus successor re-audit.

\subsection{Contract gaps}

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F005 --- P1, observed, high confidence.}
The package has no model or product for detector-relative, array/common
absolute-scale, extinction, beam/template, bandpass/color, or cross-sample
calibration covariance.  Downstream inverse variance therefore represents at
most conditional statistical uncertainty and cannot support full covariance,
absolute significance, or combined-array absolute photometry.

\emph{Closure gate:} approve the decomposition from frozen-core labels
\code{eq:log-factor-perturbation} through \code{eq:full-covariance}, define
which terms are conditioned versus propagated, persist their provenance and
correlation scope, and validate analytic and Monte Carlo covariance transfer.

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F007 --- P1, observed, high confidence.}
Requested, effective, observation-resolved, and realized provenance is not
reproducible from products.  A basename and optional copied APT columns do not
identify the exact coefficient bytes or join; \code{MEAN_TAU} does not
reconstruct time-varying correction; the pivot, factor decomposition,
flxscale correction, response Jacobian, invalid support, and systematic terms
are absent.  Map-only outputs are especially insufficient.

that a product reader can reconstruct or verify the exact realized multiplier,
identity join, target-unit basis, response, validity, and covariance inputs.

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F008 --- P1, observed, high confidence.}
The CAL-to-RTC/PTC interface does not state whether \code{responsivity},
\code{flxscale}, \code{sens}, and \code{fcf} are gains or reciprocal
responsivities, their units and normalization populations, which factors each
contains, or how replacement/filtering and weighting must propagate them.
The observed implementation applies \code{flxscale} to signal, excludes it
from \code{fcf}, combines \code{fcf} with \code{sens} in PTC, and uses
\code{responsivity} in post-calibration despike replacement.  That interface
may be internally intended, but its scientific identity is not auditable.

\emph{Closure gate:} the CAL, RTC, and PTC owners must approve a factor table
with units, reciprocal convention, normalization, covariance, lifecycle, and
response behavior; deterministic donor/target and weight-transfer fixtures
must match it.

\subsection{Scientific-policy decision}

\paragraph{SCI-CAL-001-F006 --- P1, owner decision, high confidence.}
The scientific owner must choose the authorized meaning of every output unit
and response: calibration reference pivot; per-detector versus array/common
beam; elliptical versus circularized/effective beam area; Gaussian/Airy/FITS
template normalization; nominal-frequency RJ versus bandpass/color transfer;
surface brightness, per-pixel value, per-beam amplitude, point-source flux,
and integrated flux; and how their uncertainties correlate.  More than one
choice is plausible and each changes consumer meaning.  Source comments or
current metadata are not approval.

\emph{Decision gate:} approve a bounded unit/beam/template matrix with allowed
consumers and tolerances.  Until then, non-default units and absolute or
integrated photometry fail closed.

\emph{Closure gate:} complete the falsifiable matrix in
