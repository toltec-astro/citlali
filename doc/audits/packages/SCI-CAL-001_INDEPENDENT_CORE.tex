\begin{abstract}
This pre-source core defines the calibration transformation independently of
the Citlali implementation.  It requires one explicit, provenance-bearing
factorization from detector-domain samples to a declared photometric reference,
an extinction reference, and a target unit.  It treats sample noise,
detector-relative uncertainty, array/common calibration uncertainty,
extinction uncertainty, and beam/template response uncertainty as distinct
objects.  No implementation, test, writer, configuration, provenance, or
consumer source specific to SCI-CAL-001 was inspected in deriving these bytes.
All conclusions involving sample timestamps, aligned elevation, or synthesized
samples are conditioned on the approved abstract SCI-ALIGN-001 input.
\end{abstract}

identify a detector by stable APT UID, and \(t\) identify a zero-based position
on the ordered common sample axis for that observation.  The primary TOD has

UID, detector row, and map index are distinct identities.  A calibration-table
row is joined to a detector by UID, not by row position; array and network
identities are checked as join constraints.  Exactly one eligible calibration
record is required for every selected detector.

\section{Independent estimator}

\subsection{Coefficient identity and normalization}

The effective detector coefficient may be represented directly by \(g_{oi}\)
or factorized as
\begin{equation}
  g_{oi}=G_{oa(i)} f_{oi}.
  \label{eq:gain-factorization}
\end{equation}
When a relative-flat normalization is used, its reference population
\(\mathcal R_{oa}\), weights \(r_{oi}>0\), and convention must be recorded.  A
permissible convention is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal R_{oa}} r_{oi} f_{oi}}
       {\sum_{i\in\mathcal R_{oa}} r_{oi}}=1.
  \label{eq:flat-normalization}
\end{equation}
Changing the population or weights changes \(G\) and \(f\), even if their
product is nominally similar; it is observation-resolved state, not a harmless
container reorder.  If no factorization is used, Equation~\eqref{eq:flat-normalization}
is not applicable because the complete, unit-bearing \(g_{oi}\) is the atomic
calibration coefficient; the implementation must say so rather than imply an
unknown normalization.

The coefficient sign is not assumed positive: some detector response
conventions can be signed.  It must be finite and nonzero, and the metadata
must state whether the stored quantity is gain \(U_{\rm ref}/U_x\) or
responsivity \(U_x/U_{\rm ref}\).  An inverse is taken only once at a checked
cold boundary.  Missing, duplicate, disabled, flagged, or ambiguous UID joins
invalidate the detector; row-order fallback is forbidden.

\subsection{Atmospheric extinction}

For zenith opacity and a named airmass model, define the top-of-reference
correction
\begin{equation}
  E_{oati}=
  \exp\!\left[\tau_{oat}\{X(e_{ot})-X^{\rm ref}_{oi}\}\right].
  \label{eq:extinction}
\end{equation}
The detector coefficient \(g_{oi}\) is defined at \(X^{\rm ref}_{oi}\).  Thus
\(X^{\rm ref}=0\) means the gain already targets top-of-atmosphere signal,
while \(X^{\rm ref}=1\) means it is normalized at zenith.  This explicit pivot
prevents applying extinction twice.  A coefficient derived from a calibrator
at some other airmass must record that pivot or be transformed, with
covariance, to the canonical reference.

The plane-parallel choice \(X(e)=\csc e\) is one possible model, not an
unstated default.  If selected, its derivative is
\begin{equation}
  X'(e)=-\csc e\cot e,
  \label{eq:airmass-derivative}
\end{equation}
and its approximation and low-elevation limit must be declared.  Negative
opacity, nonfinite elevation, an unbracketed time-dependent opacity, or airmass
outside the approved model range is invalid.  Clipping airmass silently would
bias amplitudes and is not allowed.

\subsection{Target-unit transfer}

Let \(U_{\rm ref}\) be the coefficient's declared photometric reference unit
and \(U_{\rm tar}\) the requested target.  The transfer \(q_{oat}\) is a unit-
bearing, provenance-bearing factor, not a label substitution.  For the common
case \(U_{\rm ref}=\mathrm{mJy/beam}\) with effective beam solid angle
\(\Omega_{\rm eff,oa}\) in sr,
\begin{align}
q_{\mathrm{mJy/beam}} &= 1,
\label{eq:unit-mjybeam}\\
q_{\mathrm{MJy/sr}} &= \frac{1}{10^{9}\Omega_{\rm eff,oa}},
\label{eq:unit-mjysr}\\
q_{\mathrm{Jy/pixel},p} &= 10^{-3}
     \frac{\Omega_{p}}{\Omega_{\rm eff,oa}},
\label{eq:unit-jypixel}\\
q_{\mathrm{uK_{CMB}}} &=
 \frac{10^{-23}}
 {\Omega_{\rm eff,oa}\,K_{a}^{\rm CMB}}
 \quad \frac{\mathrm{uK_{CMB}}}{\mathrm{mJy/beam}},
\label{eq:unit-uk}
\end{align}
where \(\Omega_p\) is the WCS-derived pixel solid angle and
\(K_a^{\rm CMB}\) is the bandpass/color-convention averaged
\(\partial B_\nu/\partial T_{\rm CMB}\) in
\(\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,Hz^{-1}\,sr^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:unit-uk} includes \(1\,\mathrm{mJy}=10^{-29}
\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,Hz^{-1}}\) and \(10^6\) microkelvin per kelvin.
It is invalid without the bandpass, reference spectrum/color convention, and
beam identity.  A Rayleigh--Jeans temperature is a different target unit and
must not be labeled \(\mathrm{uK_{CMB}}\).

The pixel transfer in Equation~\eqref{eq:unit-jypixel} is map-pixel dependent
unless a constant pixel solid angle is explicitly valid for the WCS.  It is
therefore normally a map-boundary operation, not a detector-TOD scalar.  A TOD
labeled Jy/pixel without realized pixel geometry has no defined meaning and
must fail closed.  Likewise, the effective beam is bandpass-, spectrum-,
epoch-, array-, and template-dependent.  A Gaussian approximation
\(\Omega=\pi\theta_{\rm maj}\theta_{\rm min}/(4\ln2)\) is permitted only when
that model is explicitly selected and its uncertainty is propagated.

\subsection{Calibrated signal and response companions}

Define the scalar calibration multiplier
\begin{equation}
  a_{oti}=q_{oat}\,E_{oati}\,g_{oi}.
  \label{eq:multiplier}
\end{equation}
The independently claimed affine estimator is
\begin{equation}
  y_{oti}=m_{oti}\,a_{oti}\,(x_{oti}-b_{oti}),
  \label{eq:signal-estimator}
\end{equation}
where multiplication by \(m\) is notation for domain restriction, not for
writing numerical zero into an invalid sample.  If \(b\) is fixed, this is
\(\boldsymbol y=A\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol c\), with
\(A=\diag(m a)\) on valid support and
\(\boldsymbol c=-A\boldsymbol b\).  If \(g,\tau,X,q\), or \(b\) are fitted or
derived from the same data, the unconditional estimator is nonlinear/data-
dependent and the conditional affine equations alone are insufficient.

For a declared additive response companion
\(k_{oti,r}=\partial x_{oti}/\partial\alpha_r\),
\begin{equation}
  k^{(y)}_{oti,r}=m_{oti}a_{oti}k_{oti,r}.
  \label{eq:kernel-transfer}
\end{equation}
The same selection and multiplicative operator must be applied to signal,
injections, noise realizations, and truly synthetic Jacobian companions.
Equation~\eqref{eq:kernel-transfer} does not authorize treating another
measured channel as a kernel.  A companion that is an inverse response,
variance, weight, or normalized template transforms according to its own
dimensions, not by blind reuse of this equation.

Conditioned on fixed calibration state,
\begin{align}
  \E[\boldsymbol y\mid\boldsymbol\theta]
    &=A\{\E[\boldsymbol x\mid\boldsymbol\theta]-\boldsymbol b\},
  \label{eq:conditional-mean}\\
  C_{y\mid\theta}&=A C_{x\mid\theta}A^{\mathsf T}.
  \label{eq:conditional-covariance}
\end{align}
Here \(\boldsymbol\theta\) contains every calibration, extinction, unit,
beam/template, and selected-validity quantity.  These equations preserve all
sample, detector, and observation correlations in \(C_x\).

\section{Response and normalization}

On fixed valid support, the exact local response is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial y_{oti}}{\partial x_{o't'i'}}
  =a_{oti}\,\delta_{oo'}\delta_{tt'}\delta_{ii'}.
  \label{eq:sample-response}
\end{equation}
Calibration is diagonal in the detector/sample basis and introduces no
temporal convolution, spatial remapping, or edge fill.  A constant \(a\)
preserves temporal frequency response up to amplitude.  A time-varying
extinction or unit factor is amplitude modulation; in Fourier space it mixes
frequencies and cannot be summarized as a stationary scalar transfer
function.  Any smoothing or interpolation of \(\tau\) is a separately named
operator with a response and covariance.

For a response template \(p_{oti}\) with source amplitude \(\alpha\),
\begin{equation}
 R_{oti}(p)=\frac{\partial\E[y_{oti}]}{\partial\alpha}
 =m_{oti}a_{oti}p_{oti}.
 \label{eq:template-response}
\end{equation}
Downstream linear operators must propagate Equation~\eqref{eq:template-response}
with the signal.  CAL does not define their filtering, mapmaking, or fitting
response.

For a beam \(P_a(\boldsymbol\omega)\) normalized to unit peak,
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_{\rm eff,a}=\int P_a(\boldsymbol\omega)\,d\Omega.
 \label{eq:beam-solid-angle}
\end{equation}
A map in mJy/beam then has point-source normalization: an unresolved source of
flux \(S\) mJy has ideal peak \(S\) mJy/beam, and, on complete support,
\begin{equation}
 S_{\rm int}[\mathrm{Jy}]=10^{-3}\sum_p
 M_p[\mathrm{mJy/beam}]\frac{\Omega_p}{\Omega_{\rm eff}}.
 \label{eq:integrated-flux}
\end{equation}
Surface brightness in MJy/sr, per-pixel integrated flux in Jy/pixel, a
peak-normalized point-source amplitude, and an integrated extended-source flux
are not interchangeable.  Extended or resolved-source use requires the
appropriate beam/template and aperture response, including missing support;
CAL alone cannot promise its recovery.

There is no iteration-to-iteration calibration estimator in this core.
Observation-resolved \(g,\tau,X^{\rm ref},q\), and beam identity are immutable
within an absolute reduction iteration and repeat identically when the same
observation is revisited.  An implementation that learns or rewrites them from
fruit-loop output is a different stateful estimator requiring a successor
contract.  Therefore a state-update equation is not applicable to the claimed
CAL estimator; exact replay across iterations remains an applicable lifecycle
test.

\section{Uncertainty and full covariance}

\subsection{Conditional sample covariance and inverse variance}

Equation~\eqref{eq:conditional-covariance} is the formal sample covariance
conditional on calibration state.  When only diagonal conditional variances
are retained,
\begin{equation}
 v^{(y)}_j=a_j^2v^{(x)}_j,
 \qquad
 w^{(y)}_j=\frac{w^{(x)}_j}{a_j^2},
 \label{eq:variance-weight-transfer}
\end{equation}
for compound valid index \(j=(o,t,i)\).  The full precision transformation,
when invertible on valid support, is
\begin{equation}
 W_y=A^{-\mathsf T}W_xA^{-1}.
 \label{eq:precision-transfer}
\end{equation}
An inverse variance has units \(U_{\rm tar}^{-2}\).  It is not support,
validity, hits, observing-time coverage, confidence, or response.  A zero
weight may express no statistical precision only under an explicit product
contract; it does not repair a missing calibration identity.

Empirical/jackknife realizations do not define CAL's coefficient.  If present,
each realization must receive the identical realized operator and selection
before SCI-NOI packages estimate empirical covariance.  CAL must not replace
the full conditional covariance with a diagonal weight merely because a
downstream product stores only the diagonal.

\subsection{Calibration, extinction, and response covariance}

Let \(z_j=x_j-b_j\), \(\mu_j=\E[y_j\mid\theta]\), and define small factor
perturbations.  To first order,
\begin{equation}
 \delta\ln a_j = \delta\ln g_i+\delta\ln q_j
 +(X_j-X^{\rm ref}_i)\delta\tau_j
 +\tau_j\{X'(e_j)\delta e_j-\delta X^{\rm ref}_i\}.
 \label{eq:log-factor-perturbation}
\end{equation}
Beam size, bandpass, color convention, pixel area, and template parameters
enter through \(\delta\ln q\).  Define
\(H_{jr}=\partial\ln a_j/\partial\theta_r\).  The calibration/systematic
contribution is
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm cal}\simeq \diag(\boldsymbol\mu)H C_\theta H^{\mathsf T}
                    \diag(\boldsymbol\mu).
 \label{eq:calibration-covariance}
\end{equation}
It is generally dense.  A global absolute-scale term produces rank-one
correlation across all affected samples; an array term correlates detectors
and times within that array; detector-flat terms correlate all samples for one
detector; a shared opacity estimate correlates samples according to airmass.
These terms must not be added independently to every pixel and then inverted
as though they were white noise.

With uncertain baselines and possible dependence between raw data and fitted
calibration state, the first-order full covariance is
\begin{align}
 C_y \simeq{}& A C_x A^{\mathsf T}
 +J_\theta C_\theta J_\theta^{\mathsf T}
 +J_b C_b J_b^{\mathsf T} \\
 &+A C_{x\theta}J_\theta^{\mathsf T}
 +J_\theta C_{\theta x}A^{\mathsf T}
 +\text{baseline and higher-order cross terms},
 \label{eq:full-covariance}
\end{align}
where \(J_\theta=\partial\E[\boldsymbol y]/\partial\boldsymbol\theta\) and
\(J_b=-A\).  Cross terms may be set to zero only with an explicit independence
argument.  Large or asymmetric opacity uncertainty requires nonlinear
propagation or posterior samples because the exponential can make the
linearized mean and covariance biased.  Selection uncertainty is likewise not
captured by conditioning on a fixed mask; threshold-sensitive cases require
resampling or a bounded selection study.

Conditional formal noise, empirical noise, detector-relative calibration
uncertainty, common absolute scale, extinction uncertainty, and beam/template
response uncertainty must be published as separable components or as a
documented covariance/nuisance model.  If only conditional statistical weight
is available, metadata must say explicitly that it excludes calibration and
response systematics.

\section{Validity, non-finite policy, and lifecycle}

\subsection{Validity and failure rules}

An output sample is scientifically valid only if all of the following hold:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item SCI-ALIGN-001 marks the sample eligible and supplies a valid timestamp
and aligned elevation with explicit original/synthesized identity;
\item the detector is selected, its UID join is unique, and all relevant APT
and detector flags permit use;
\item \(x-b\), \(g\), \(\tau\), \(X\), \(X^{\rm ref}\), and \(q\) are finite and
inside their declared domains;
\item the opacity validity interval covers the sample and the atmospheric
model covers the elevation/airmass;
\item every required beam, bandpass, color, template, and pixel identity for
the requested unit is resolved; and
\item the resulting sample and required uncertainty components are finite.
\end{enumerate}

Missing, disabled, automatic, and unavailable are semantic states, not NaN,
zero, negative, or stale-value sentinels.  Invalid detectors/samples are
flagged and excluded; multiplying them to zero would create false sky signal
and support.  A required observation fails closed when no approved
calibration/extinction/unit path exists or when remaining support violates a
predeclared minimum.  The implementation may not inherit coefficients, tau,
beam state, or source context from a preceding observation.

Interpolated/synthesized samples retain that identity.  Whether they are
eligible, and which timestamp/elevation they carry, is owned by SCI-ALIGN-001.
CAL applies the same declared per-sample operator only to those marked
eligible; it does not erase interpolation provenance or independently invent a
gap policy.

\section{Analytic limits and pre-registered falsification tests}

All seven standard evidence methods are applicable.  None is omitted by tier.
The tolerances below are fixed before source inspection; a required-data skip
is not a pass.

\subsection{Analytic limits}

\begin{enumerate}[label=CAL-A\arabic*,leftmargin=*]
\item With \(g=q=1\), \(\tau=0\), \(b=0\), and all-valid support,
Equation~\eqref{eq:signal-estimator} is the identity, the covariance is
unchanged, and a synthetic kernel is unchanged.
\item At the coefficient's atmospheric pivot \(X=X^{\rm ref}\), extinction is
exactly one for every finite tau.  With top-of-atmosphere pivot zero and zenith
airmass one, \(E=\exp(\tau)\).
\item For constant scalar \(a\), signal and kernel scale by \(a\), variance by
\(a^2\), and inverse variance by \(a^{-2}\); flags, sample counts, and coverage
do not change.
\item Reordering calibration rows while retaining UID identity leaves every
output invariant.  Duplicating, deleting, or mismatching a UID fails or flags
the affected detector according to the declared required-support policy.
\item mJy/beam \(\leftrightarrow\) MJy/sr and mJy/beam
\(\leftrightarrow\) Jy/pixel round trips are identity to floating-point error
when the same \(\Omega_{\rm eff}\) and \(\Omega_p\) are used.  Changing beam
solid angle changes surface brightness and integrated flux but not the
point-source peak convention.
\item A single common fractional gain uncertainty \(s\) produces
\(C_{\rm cal}=s^2\boldsymbol\mu\boldsymbol\mu^{\mathsf T}\), a rank-one matrix;
it does not decrease with more samples.
\item Zero input with zero baseline remains zero for all finite multiplicative
factors.  Calibration creates no additive offset.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Synthetic injection and blank controls}

A synthetic point-source template and a uniform extended-brightness template
are attenuated by known array-dependent gains and opacity over at least three
airmasses, then calibrated.  TOD-domain recovered response must equal
Equation~\eqref{eq:template-response}; after an independently contracted map
operator, the point-source peak and extended/integrated meanings must satisfy
Equations~\eqref{eq:beam-solid-angle}--\eqref{eq:integrated-flux}.  Deterministic
injections use the CAL-T1 tolerance.  Seeded noisy injections require the
mean normalized residual over 1000 realizations to be within three Monte Carlo
standard errors of zero and the covariance Frobenius residual to be within
five Monte Carlo standard errors of Equation~\eqref{eq:full-covariance}.

Zero-signal and source-free Gaussian blank controls are required because a
purely multiplicative calibration must not create an offset or airmass trend.
The fitted mean must be within three predicted standard errors of zero; a
linear residual-versus-airmass slope must be within three predicted standard
errors of zero.  Blank empirical covariance is compared separately with the
conditional sample covariance; injected common calibration nuisances must
recover the predicted dense covariance rather than being absorbed into white
weight.

\subsection{Same-SHA external and astronomical evidence}

A human-run Unity campaign is required at the exact governing SHA because
local analytic fixtures cannot establish external build/runtime behavior or
astronomical recovery.  It must return immutable input/config identifiers,
APT/calibration/tau/beam/bandpass sources, observation IDs, timestamps,
command, build/compiler/runtime identity, thread count, complete logs,
products, realized provenance, and SHA-256 hashes.  It must include sequential
and production-thread runs for one Pointing/OOF, one Science, and one Beammap
case spanning all present arrays and at least two materially different
airmasses.  Exact product inventory and zero unexpected error-level messages
are gates; CAL-resolved values, unit metadata, flags, and applicable
elementwise results must meet the deterministic gates above.

Astronomical recovery uses independently predicted standard-source fluxes or
surface brightnesses with epoch, bandpass, color, beam/template, and
uncertainty provenance.  For each valid measurement the normalized residual
\((S_{\rm rec}-S_{\rm ref})/\sigma_{\rm total}\) must have magnitude at most
three.  Within each array, the inverse-covariance weighted mean residual must
have magnitude at most two standard errors, and the recovered/reference ratio
versus airmass must have a slope consistent with zero within two standard
errors.  The covariance includes shared reference, array calibration,
extinction, and beam terms, so correlated standards are not counted as
independent.  These thresholds do not authorize production; they are
falsification gates for the claimed response.
