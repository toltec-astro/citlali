\section{Bounded scientific claim}

For observation $o$, detector $d$, and stable detector-reference sample $s$,
AST accepts an approved astrometric plan and abstract aligned inputs.  It
returns a realized coordinate record
\[
 \mathcal{A}_{ods} =
 \{\widehat{\boldsymbol r}_{ods}^{F},\,\boldsymbol w_{ods},\,
   \boldsymbol q_{ods},\,\nu_{ods},\,\mathcal{W}_{o},\,\mathcal{U}_{ods},
   \mathcal{P}_{ods}\},
\]
where $F$ is the declared output frame, $\widehat{\boldsymbol r}$ is a unit
direction, $\boldsymbol w$ is a continuous tangent-plane coordinate,
$\boldsymbol q$ is a continuous FITS pixel coordinate, $\nu$ is explicit
validity and eligibility, $\mathcal W_o$ is immutable persisted WCS identity,
$\mathcal U$ is the uncertainty/response availability bundle, and
$\mathcal P$ is provenance.  Discrete map accumulation, source fitting,
pointing/OOF fitting, Beammap fitting, photometry, filtering, and significance
are outside this estimator.

TolTECA is authoritative for selecting pointing-support records.  AST may
validate and apply supplied records; it must not select a calibrator, search
for replacement records, or reinterpret missing upstream authority as zero.

\section{Spherical coordinate identities}

\subsection{Unit vectors, local basis, and wrap}

For longitude $\ell$ and latitude $b$ in any declared spherical frame, define
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol r(\ell,b)=
 \begin{bmatrix}\cos b\cos\ell\\ \cos b\sin\ell\\ \sin b\end{bmatrix},
 \quad
 \boldsymbol e_{\ell}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\ell\\\cos\ell\\0\end{bmatrix},
 \quad
 \boldsymbol e_b=
 \begin{bmatrix}-\sin b\cos\ell\\-\sin b\sin\ell\\\cos b\end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:unit-vector}
\end{equation}
$\boldsymbol e_{\ell}$ points toward increasing longitude at fixed latitude;
$\boldsymbol e_b$ points toward increasing latitude.  Both are unit tangent
vectors away from the coordinate pole.  The vector $\boldsymbol r$, not a
longitude value, remains the identity at a pole.

For period $P$, circular difference and normalized output are
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{wrap}_P(\theta)=
 \theta-P\left\lfloor\frac{\theta+P/2}{P}\right\rfloor\in[-P/2,P/2),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{norm}_P(\theta)=\theta-P\left\lfloor\frac{\theta}{P}\right\rfloor
 \in[0,P).
 \label{eq:wrap}
\end{equation}
RA and azimuth differences use $P=2\pi$.  Ordinary subtraction across the
zero meridian is a falsifying implementation.

\subsection{Exact tangent displacement}

For tangent components $\boldsymbol\eta=(\eta_{\ell},\eta_b)^\T$ in radians,
$\rho=\|\boldsymbol\eta\|$, and
$\boldsymbol u=(\eta_{\ell}\boldsymbol e_{\ell}+\eta_b\boldsymbol e_b)/\rho$,
the exact spherical exponential map is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Exp}_{\boldsymbol r}(\boldsymbol\eta)=
 \begin{cases}
 \cos\rho\,\boldsymbol r+\sin\rho\,\boldsymbol u,&\rho>0,\\
 \boldsymbol r,&\rho=0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:exp-map}
\end{equation}
The output is renormalized only to remove floating-point roundoff, never to
hide non-finite or invalid input.  Directly adding longitude and latitude is a
small-angle approximation and is not the authority for this contract.

An input called an ``azimuth offset'' or ``RA offset'' must declare whether it
is a coordinate increment $\Delta\ell$ or an on-sky tangent component
$\eta_{\ell}$.  The normalization is
\begin{equation}
 \eta_{\ell}=\begin{cases}
  \cos b\,\Delta\ell,&\text{declared coordinate increment},\\
  \Delta\ell_{\rm tangent},&\text{declared tangent offset}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:longitude-topology}
\end{equation}
Conflating these cases is an altitude/declination-dependent scale error.

\section{Pointing correction support and sign}

\subsection{Sign and unit}

Let $\kappa=\pi/(180\,3600)$ radians per arcsecond.  After the topology
normalization in Equation~\eqref{eq:longitude-topology}, the supplied pair
$\boldsymbol a_k=(a_{\ell,k},a_{b,k})^\T$ in arcsec becomes
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol c_k=\kappa\boldsymbol a_k.
 \label{eq:offset-unit}
\end{equation}
Positive $c_{\ell}$ moves the corrected direction along increasing declared
longitude; positive $c_b$ moves it along increasing latitude.  The upstream
value must be identified as a correction with this sign.  A measured residual
with the opposite action is not silently negated; it requires an explicit
adapter and provenance.  Ambiguous sign fails before observation state is
mutated.

\subsection{Constant and two-support realization}

For a single finite pair, the correction is observation-constant:
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol c_s=\boldsymbol c_0.
 \label{eq:constant-offset}
\end{equation}
For two support pairs with finite positive MJD $m_0<m_1$, let the aligned
sample have finite MJD $m_s$.  Bracket-only interpolation is
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_s=\frac{m_s-m_0}{m_1-m_0},\qquad
 \boldsymbol c_s=(1-\lambda_s)\boldsymbol c_0+\lambda_s\boldsymbol c_1,
 \qquad 0\le\lambda_s\le1.
 \label{eq:mjd-interpolation}
\end{equation}
Every AST-eligible sample must be bracketed.  Clamping $\lambda_s$, choosing
the nearest endpoint outside support, or extrapolating is forbidden.

The compatibility observation-span fallback is a distinct, explicitly
resolved policy.  It may be selected only when both support times are
intentionally absent under the documented legacy boundary and the ordered
pair is explicitly interpreted as observation endpoints.  With first and
last eligible detector-reference times $t_-$ and $t_+$,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_s^{\rm span}=\frac{t_s-t_-}{t_+-t_-},\qquad
 \boldsymbol c_s=(1-\lambda_s^{\rm span})\boldsymbol c_0
                    +\lambda_s^{\rm span}\boldsymbol c_1,
 \quad t_-\le t_s\le t_+.
 \label{eq:span-interpolation}
\end{equation}
The realized provenance names \texttt{legacy\_observation\_span}, the exact
sample identities defining $t_-$ and $t_+$, and why MJD support was absent.
Mixed positive/nonpositive support, non-finite support, reversed pairs, or
$t_+=t_-$ with unequal corrections is ambiguous and fails.  A one-sample
observation is allowed only when the two endpoint corrections are exactly
equal, reducing to Equation~\eqref{eq:constant-offset}.

\subsection{Support covariance}

Stack $\boldsymbol c_*=(\boldsymbol c_0^\T,\boldsymbol c_1^\T)^\T$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf H_s=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\lambda_s)\mathsf I_2&
                              \lambda_s\mathsf I_2\end{bmatrix},\qquad
 \boldsymbol c_s=\mathsf H_s\boldsymbol c_*,\qquad
 \mathsf C_{c,s}^{\rm formal}=\mathsf H_s\mathsf C_{c,*}\mathsf H_s^\T.
 \label{eq:support-covariance}
\end{equation}
Cross-covariance between supports is retained when supplied.  Treating the
supports as independent without authority is forbidden.  Interpolation-model,
selection, and pointing-calibrator systematics remain separate terms.  If an
input term is unavailable, the corresponding output is \Unavailable, not a
zero matrix.

\section{Boresight, correction, and detector composition}

\subsection{Order of operations}

Let $\boldsymbol r^H_s$ be the aligned telescope boresight in the local
horizon frame.  The corrected boresight is
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol r^{H,c}_s=
 \operatorname{Exp}_{\boldsymbol r^H_s}(\boldsymbol c_s).
 \label{eq:corrected-boresight}
\end{equation}
Let $\boldsymbol f_d$ be the calibrated detector focal-plane offset and
$\mathsf B_{ds}$ the declared two-dimensional map from its basis to the local
tangent basis at $\boldsymbol r^{H,c}_s$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\xi_{ds}=\mathsf B_{ds}\boldsymbol f_d,
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol r^H_{ds}=
 \operatorname{Exp}_{\boldsymbol r^{H,c}_s}(\boldsymbol\xi_{ds}).
 \label{eq:detector-composition}
\end{equation}
Correction first and detector offset second is the physical AST convention.
If an upstream detector direction already includes either operation, its
composition history must declare that fact so the operation is not applied
twice.  A detector join uses stable UID and the full observation detector
identity, not a dense position inherited from another product.

The small-angle relation
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol\eta_{ds}\simeq\boldsymbol c_s+\mathsf B_{ds}\boldsymbol f_d
 \label{eq:small-angle}
\end{equation}
is an analytic limit and a test oracle near the center.  It is not authority
to replace Equations~\eqref{eq:corrected-boresight} and
\eqref{eq:detector-composition} without a bounded-error decision.

\subsection{AltAz and J2000 output}

Point, OOF, and Beammap tangent coordinates use $\boldsymbol r^H_{ds}$ and an
AltAz center.  Science uses an explicitly identified equatorial J2000
direction
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol r^{J2000}_{ds}=
 \mathsf R_{J2000\leftarrow H,s}\boldsymbol r^H_{ds}.
 \label{eq:frame-transform}
\end{equation}
$\mathsf R$ is a proper rotation or a more complete named astrometric
operator when refraction or non-rotational corrections apply.  Its inputs and
version include time scale, observatory position, Earth orientation,
precession/nutation and aberration policy, and refraction policy.  If the
application instead accepts an authoritative aligned J2000 boresight, the
provenance must show the equivalent correction and focal-plane-basis path.
Two competing boresight authorities cannot be silently mixed.

``J2000'', ``ICRS'', apparent place, observed AltAz, and unrefracted AltAz are
not synonyms.  If the exact equatorial frame or transformation model is
unavailable, coordinates may remain usable only under an explicitly
restricted compatibility identity; absolute J2000 precision and its
covariance fail closed.

\section{Tangent projection, WCS, and pixelization}

\subsection{Gnomonic forward and inverse maps}

For target direction $\boldsymbol r$, center $\boldsymbol r_0$, and the
center basis $(\boldsymbol e_1,\boldsymbol e_2)$ pointing toward increasing
longitude and latitude, define
\begin{equation}
 D=\boldsymbol r\cdot\boldsymbol r_0,\qquad
 x=\frac{\boldsymbol r\cdot\boldsymbol e_1}{D},\qquad
 y=\frac{\boldsymbol r\cdot\boldsymbol e_2}{D},\qquad D>0.
 \label{eq:tan-forward}
\end{equation}
The inverse is
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol r=
 \frac{\boldsymbol r_0+x\boldsymbol e_1+y\boldsymbol e_2}
      {\sqrt{1+x^2+y^2}}.
 \label{eq:tan-inverse}
\end{equation}
For $(\ell,b)$ and center $(\ell_0,b_0)$, use the wrapped
$\Delta\ell=\operatorname{wrap}_{2\pi}(\ell-\ell_0)$:
\begin{align}
 D &= \sin b_0\sin b+\cos b_0\cos b\cos\Delta\ell,\nonumber\\
 x &= \frac{\cos b\sin\Delta\ell}{D},\qquad
 y = \frac{\cos b_0\sin b-\sin b_0\cos b\cos\Delta\ell}{D}.
 \label{eq:tan-lonlat}
\end{align}
The center maps exactly to $(0,0)$.  Directions with non-finite $D$ or
$D\le D_{\min}>0$ are out of the approved projection domain.  They are never
clamped to an edge pixel.

For Point/OOF and Beammap, the plane values are
$\boldsymbol w=206264.806247\,(x,y)^\T$ arcsec and the axis identity is
\texttt{AZOFFSET}, \texttt{ELOFFSET}.  For science,
$\boldsymbol w=(180/\pi)(x,y)^\T$ degrees and the axes are equatorial
\texttt{RA---TAN}, \texttt{DEC--TAN}.  The center frame and identity remain
part of $\mathcal W_o$.

\subsection{Continuous WCS and handedness}

Let FITS pixel coordinates be $\boldsymbol q=(q_1,q_2)^\T$, the one-based
reference pixel be $\boldsymbol q_0=\mathrm{CRPIX}$, and let $\mathsf M$ be
the declared non-singular $2\times2$ CD matrix in plane-units per pixel:
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol w=\mathsf M(\boldsymbol q-\boldsymbol q_0),\qquad
 \boldsymbol q=\boldsymbol q_0+\mathsf M^{-1}\boldsymbol w.
 \label{eq:wcs-linear}
\end{equation}
The projection center is \texttt{CRVAL}; $\mathsf M$ or an exactly equivalent
\texttt{PC}$+$\texttt{CDELT} representation fixes scale, rotation, axis order,
sign, and handedness.  The determinant sign is persisted; consumers use the
WCS rather than array display orientation.  The canonical north-up,
east-left science display has $M_{11}<0$, $M_{22}>0$, off-diagonal zeros, and
$\det\mathsf M<0$.  Any other realized orientation is admissible only when
requested/effective, explicit in $\mathsf M$, and verified by directional
tests.  Hidden flips or transposes are defects.

For $N_1$ columns and $N_2$ rows with the projection center at the geometric
array center,
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{CRPIX}_1=\frac{N_1+1}{2},\qquad
 \mathrm{CRPIX}_2=\frac{N_2+1}{2}.
 \label{eq:crpix}
\end{equation}
Even dimensions therefore have half-integer reference pixels.  If a different
reference pixel is realized, Equation~\eqref{eq:wcs-linear} must still place
\texttt{CRVAL} there exactly and provenance must explain the center policy.

\subsection{Zero-based memory and discrete assignment}

Memory column $j$ and row $i$ are zero-based, while FITS coordinates are
\begin{equation}
 q_1=j+1,qquad q_2=i+1
 \label{eq:index-base}
\end{equation}
at pixel centers.  Given continuous $\boldsymbol q$, half-open nearest-center
assignment is
\begin{equation}
 j=\left\lfloor q_1-\tfrac12\right\rfloor,qquad
 i=\left\lfloor q_2-\tfrac12\right\rfloor,qquad
 0\le j<N_1,\quad0\le i<N_2.
 \label{eq:pixel-assignment}
\end{equation}
The lower edge is included and the upper edge excluded.  A different bin
policy is possible only as an explicitly versioned operator with equivalent
boundary tests; it cannot be inferred from integer casting.  Continuous WCS
round trip, discrete in-bounds selection, and map coverage are distinct facts.

\section{Response and uncertainty propagation}

\subsection{Differential response}

For Equation~\eqref{eq:tan-forward}, with
$a=\boldsymbol r\cdot\boldsymbol e_1$ and
$b=\boldsymbol r\cdot\boldsymbol e_2$, the direction-to-plane Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf J_{w\leftarrow r}=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 (D\boldsymbol e_1^\T-a\boldsymbol r_0^\T)/D^2\\
 (D\boldsymbol e_2^\T-b\boldsymbol r_0^\T)/D^2
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \label{eq:projection-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The unit conversion multiplies this matrix.  Continuous pixel response is
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf J_{q\leftarrow z}=\mathsf M^{-1}
 \mathsf J_{w\leftarrow r}\mathsf J_{r\leftarrow z},
 \label{eq:pixel-jacobian}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol z$ contains boresight, support corrections, detector
offsets, basis orientation, frame-transform parameters, and projection center.
Center uncertainty requires derivatives of $\boldsymbol r_0$ and its basis;
it is not omitted merely because \texttt{CRVAL} is stored.

The Jacobians of Equations~\eqref{eq:exp-map},
\eqref{eq:detector-composition}, and \eqref{eq:frame-transform} may be
implemented analytically, by validated automatic differentiation, or by a
pre-registered finite-difference oracle.  Their method/version is realized
provenance.  At zero offset and the projection center, the tangent response
must approach the identity after declared unit and axis-sign conversions.

\subsection{Covariance and unavailable terms}

Covariance is propagated by the same realized operator:
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf C_y^{(k)}=\mathsf J_{y\leftarrow z_k}
 \mathsf C_{z_k}^{(k)}\mathsf J_{y\leftarrow z_k}^{\T},
 \label{eq:cov-component}
\end{equation}
for each available component $k$.  The reported bundle keeps distinct
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf C_y^{\rm formal},\quad
 \mathsf C_y^{\rm pointing\,cal},\quad
 \mathsf C_y^{\rm focal\,plane\,cal},\quad
 \mathsf C_y^{\rm frame/model},\quad
 \mathsf C_y^{\rm interpolation},\quad
 \mathsf C_y^{\rm systematic}.
 \label{eq:cov-total}
\end{equation}
A total may be formed only when cross-covariances and combination assumptions
are declared.  Selection/availability uncertainty is generally a discrete or
mixture uncertainty and is not converted to Gaussian covariance without an
approved model.  Pointing-calibrator fit uncertainty is an upstream support
term; source-fit uncertainty is downstream and must not be substituted for it.

Pixelization response is the deterministic cell operator of
Equation~\eqref{eq:pixel-assignment}.  If a downstream analysis approximates
an unknown within-pixel location by the pixel center, the uniform-cell term
$\mathsf M\mathsf M^\T/12$ is a separately labeled quantization model, not
formal astrometric covariance.  Projection response, pixel support, validity,
coverage, calibrator uncertainty, source-fit uncertainty, and map-domain
significance remain separate quantities.

No full observation covariance is required when its inputs do not exist.
The availability manifest must say whether each mean, covariance, systematic,
interpolation, frame-model, selection, projection-response, and pixelization
term is \texttt{available}, \texttt{available\_conditional},
\texttt{unavailable\_input}, \NA, or \texttt{not\_persisted\_standard}.

\subsection{Observation lifecycle and parallel equivalence}

Observation resolution constructs a complete immutable candidate
$\mathcal A_o$ off to the side.  Only after all required fields, supports,
detector joins, frame operators, and WCS checks pass may it atomically replace
the previous observation state.  At every observation boundary, absent
optional state is explicitly reset to disabled/unavailable.  Failure leaves no
candidate usable by the next observation.

Equations~\eqref{eq:mjd-interpolation}--\eqref{eq:pixel-assignment} are pure in
$(o,d,s)$ and immutable observation state.  Sequential and parallel execution
must produce the same coordinates, validity, detector identity, discrete
pixel, counts, and provenance ordering under the declared numerical policy.
Parallel workers may not update shared AST state, choose a center, or observe a
partially replaced correction plan.

Simulation and alternate compiled paths use the same equations and state
machine.  A simulated boresight, detector geometry, or correction is labeled
synthetic with exact generator/version/seed.  Simulation cannot bypass
support, wrap, index, validity, or output-failure checks.  An alternate path
that emits different coordinate meaning is a separate estimator and fails
this contract until approved.
