\subsection{Correction support and spherical composition}

The one-support case is constant.  For two valid supports
$(\tau_0,\boldsymbol\delta_0)$ and
$(\tau_1,\boldsymbol\delta_1)$, the proposed realization is
\begin{align}
 \alpha(t)&=\frac{t-\tau_0}{\tau_1-\tau_0}, &
 \boldsymbol\delta(t)&=(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol\delta_0+
                         \alpha\boldsymbol\delta_1,
 \label{eq:audit-interp}
\end{align}
only for $\tau_0\le t\le\tau_1$.  Observation-span interpolation is a distinct
explicit policy using the first and last valid observation time; a mixed
sentinel is not a third implicit mode.  The full covariance includes the
support covariance and, when support time is uncertain, the derivative with
respect to support time.

Let $\boldsymbol e_E,\boldsymbol e_N$ be the declared local east/north tangent
basis.  After applying the approved correction sign on the sphere, detector
composition is
\begin{align}
 \widehat{\boldsymbol b}'_{os}
  &=\operatorname{Exp}_{\widehat{\boldsymbol b}_{os}}
       \left(J_{\rm corr}\boldsymbol\delta_{os}\right),\\
 \widehat{\boldsymbol r}_{ods}
  &=\operatorname{Exp}_{\widehat{\boldsymbol b}'_{os}}
       \left(R_{\rm fp}(o,s)\boldsymbol f_d\right),
 \label{eq:audit-compose}
\end{align}
followed by the versioned transform $T_{A\to F}$.  A small-angle additive
implementation is an approximation to Eq.~\eqref{eq:audit-compose}; it requires
an approved maximum radius and measured error bound.  Composition order,
correction sign, focal-plane basis, elevation/parallactic rotation signs, and
longitude topology are scientific identity, not implementation trivia.

\subsection{Projection, WCS, response, and uncertainty}

For center $(\lambda_0,\phi_0)$ and direction $(\lambda,\phi)$,
\begin{align}
 D &= \sin\phi\sin\phi_0+
      \cos\phi\cos\phi_0\cos(\lambda-\lambda_0),\\
 x &= \frac{\cos\phi\sin(\lambda-\lambda_0)}{D}, &
 y &= \frac{\cos\phi_0\sin\phi-
       \sin\phi_0\cos\phi\cos(\lambda-\lambda_0)}{D}.
 \label{eq:audit-tan}
\end{align}
The forward map is valid only on an owner-approved subset of $D>0$; $D\le0$,
non-finite input, or an out-of-domain radius produces explicit invalidity, not
$(0,0)$.  Inverse TAN normalizes longitude according to the declared topology.

Internal continuous pixels obey
\begin{align}
 q_1 &= q_{1,0}+x/s_1, & q_2 &= q_{2,0}+y/s_2,
 \label{eq:audit-wcs}
\end{align}
with zero-based $q_{k,0}=(N_k-1)/2$ for centered odd maps.  FITS writes add one
to CRPIX.  The current compatibility orientation stores internal longitude in
increasing column order, reverses the image x axis on write, and uses negative
CDELT1; CDELT2 is positive.  These transformations must round-trip together.

The astrometric response is the Jacobian $J=\partial(x,y)/\partial\theta$ (or
the composition of correction, frame, TAN, and pixel Jacobians).  For any
available input covariance $C_\theta$,
\begin{equation}
 C_q=J_qJ\,C_\theta\,(J_qJ)^{\T}+C_{\rm systematic}+C_{\rm response}.
 \label{eq:audit-cov}
\end{equation}
Selection-conditioning and missing covariance terms remain explicit.
Formal fit error, correction covariance, support, coordinate validity, hits,
coverage, confidence, and response are not synonyms.  If covariance is
unavailable, the product says so; it does not publish an incomplete diagonal as
the full object.

\section{Exact implementation operator}

\subsection{Pointing-support realization}

The governing source validates one or two finite az/alt values of equal length
and a two-element finite MJD vector.  One value is expanded over the telescope
sample count. For two values it chooses explicit-MJD interpolation only when
both MJD elements are positive; otherwise it silently chooses observation-span
interpolation. Explicit MJD supports are converted to \code{long long} Unix
seconds, checked for order and observation bracketing, and passed to
\code{mlinterp::interp}.  The span path uses the first and last TelTime samples
without a local strict-monotonicity/nondegeneracy check.

Thus the implemented support operator is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\boldsymbol\delta}(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  \boldsymbol\delta_0,&n=1,\\
  \operatorname{mlinterp}(t; t_{\rm first},t_{\rm last},
       \boldsymbol\delta_0,\boldsymbol\delta_1),&n=2,\ \neg(M_0>0\land M_1>0),\\
  \operatorname{mlinterp}(t;\lfloor u(M_0)\rfloor,\lfloor u(M_1)\rfloor,
       \boldsymbol\delta_0,\boldsymbol\delta_1),&n=2,\ M_0>0\land M_1>0,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:impl-interp}
\end{equation}
where $u(M)$ is Unix seconds.  At MJD 60000.123456789 the conversion loses
0.6665694714 seconds.  Mixed positive/nonpositive support is accepted as the
span mode rather than rejected as ambiguous. Duplicate axes select the last
entry with a warning; unknown entries and positional fallbacks are also warning
paths. These operations do not preserve the proposed authority distinctions.

\subsection{Boresight and detector coordinates}

RA/Dec and Galactic boresight positions use the forward gnomonic formula in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:audit-tan}.  AltAz instead uses the small-angle approximation
\begin{align}
 y_{\rm alt}&=E_{\rm act}-E_{\rm src}-E_{\rm cor},\\
 x_{\rm az}&=\cos(E_{\rm act}-E_{\rm cor})
              (A_{\rm act}-A_{\rm src})-A_{\rm cor}.
 \label{eq:impl-altaz}
\end{align}
Before subtraction it corrects only a positive difference larger than
$0.9\,2\pi$.  The independent periodic preprocessing operates on each series
only when that series itself spans the boundary.  A constant
$A_{\rm act}=0.1$, $A_{\rm src}=6.2$, $E=0.8$ control therefore produces
$x=-4.249910927$ rad, or $-876607.05$ arcsec, rather than the shortest
angular difference.

APT offsets $(x_t,y_t)$ in arcsec are rotated using elevation and then the
astrometry correction is added:
\begin{align}
 a_d &= \cos E\,x_t-\sin E\,y_t+\delta_{\rm az},\\
 e_d &= \cos E\,y_t+\sin E\,x_t+\delta_{\rm alt}.
 \label{eq:impl-fp}
\end{align}
RA/Dec and Galactic branches rotate $(a_d,e_d)$ by parallactic or combined
angles and add them linearly to the tangent boresight coordinates; AltAz adds
them to Eq.~\eqref{eq:impl-altaz}. This is a useful small-field approximation,
but the governing contract does not declare its sign, basis, approximation
radius, error, or covariance.

The APT reader requires a fixed column list and literal \code{Radesys=altaz},
then filters rows by network while retaining APT order. Setup validates group
columns, contiguity, and some beam quantities, but not $x_t/y_t$ length or
finiteness, UID uniqueness, or exact UID/order correspondence with raw detector
rows. Index $d$ is used directly by all coordinate consumers.

\subsection{TAN domain, inverse map, and validity}

The forward projection tests \code{abs(D) < epsilon}; when true it writes
$x=y=0$. It accepts every $D<0$. Consequently an exact quarter turn becomes
the finite center, one adjacent double produces $x=3.5301143212171575\times
10^{15}$, the next adjacent double again becomes center, and the antipode is
approximately center. Invalid sky direction and valid source center are thus
indistinguishable downstream.

The inverse formula is algebraically the standard inverse TAN in ordinary
finite cases, but it has no explicit finite/domain policy, does not clamp the
\code{asin} argument, and does not normalize returned longitude. Consumers
usually test only numerical bounds or finiteness. A singular direction already
aliased to finite center passes those defenses.

Map geometry takes raw min/max over detector coordinates; mapmakers form
$q=y/s+(N_y-1)/2$, $q=x/s+(N_x-1)/2$ and call \code{llround} without a common
AST coordinate-validity mask. Signal flags are not coordinate validity. This
is a concrete propagation path from AST into RTC/PTC/MAP/source selection,
Point/OOF, Beammap, and persisted TOD products.

\subsection{WCS and persisted identity}

Map configuration accepts and serializes \code{crpix1}, \code{crpix2},
\code{crval1_J2000}, \code{crval2_J2000}, \code{tan_ra}, and
\code{tan_dec}. The adapter copies CRPIX and stores CRVAL in
\code{crval_config}; geometry later overwrites CRPIX with the centered value,
observation setup always assigns WCS CRVAL from the telescope source header,
and the configured CRVAL/TAN fields have no realization path. A finite
nondefault request can therefore succeed while its scientific values are
ignored.

The generated WCS has the following positive controls: internal zero-based
CRPIX is converted to one-based FITS CRPIX, dimensions are made odd, x is
reversed on FITS write/read, CDELT1 is negative, and CDELT2 positive. Those
paths conform to the frozen compatibility orientation.

Other WCS identity does not conform. The WCS container stores CDELT/CRPIX/CRVAL
as \code{float}; 359.99999 degrees rounds to 360 degrees, a 0.036 arcsec
change. Missing or invalid source epoch warns and defaults to 2000 without a
coordinate transform. The primary \code{RADESYS} value is the internal pixel
axis label such as \code{radec}, not a standard celestial system, and image
extensions do not carry a complete approved frame identity. Fruit-loop map
admission compares absolute CDELT, discarding handedness, and permits legacy
missing RADESYS. These are incompatible with a precision WCS claim.

\subsection{Response, covariance, state, products, and simulations}

The AST operator publishes no coordinate Jacobian, correction covariance,
focal-plane covariance, alignment covariance, frame/systematic term, response
uncertainty, or selection-conditioning term. Point/source means are converted
through inverse TAN, but their errors are only multiplied by arcsec-to-degree.
At the projection center, a one-arcsecond tangent-x error corresponds to
approximately 2 arcsec of RA at declination 60 degrees and 5.75877 arcsec at
80 degrees; the implementation reports 1 arcsec. Cross-covariance is not
persisted.

The astrometry execution plan correctly distinguishes requested/effective
objects structurally and enforces nonzero matched installation/application
counts. However effective is a copy of requested, resolution contains only the
mode and explicit-MJD boolean, and realized state contains only counts. The
atomic sidecar identifies axes, arcsec, MJD, an algorithm label and authorities,
but not sign/basis/frame/topology, resolved support/sample values, APT/raw
identity, coordinate validity, WCS/products, covariance availability, or
digests. Count equality does not prove value identity.

Full TOD output labels detector coordinates in radians and pointing offsets in
arcsec. It also labels \emph{every} telescope data variable in radians,
including time, state, and counter fields. Mini TOD omits detector coordinates.
There is no coordinate-validity variable. Required output and provenance write
failures do propagate, which is positive failure-flow evidence.

Simulation skips UTC conversion, periodic preprocessing, and Galactic source
conversion; manually sets epoch 2000; and does not construct Galactic tangent
coordinates while Galactic pixel axes remain an exposed mode. Its time/support
assumptions and parity are not proven. Simulation output cannot validate the
production coordinate operator in this state.
